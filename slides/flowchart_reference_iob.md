# Flowchart reference: IAAC "Internet of Buildings" (tutor's pick, 24 Oct)

Source: https://iob.iaac.net/app.html. Each research cluster (Urban Morphology, Environmental Ecology, Energy Exchange, Mobility, Social Economy) has three panels: **Challenge → Methodology → Proposal**, each one full-screen image with a single short paragraph under it. The images are at `https://iob.iaac.net/images/<name>.png`, e.g. `EE_Methodology.png`.

## Closest to us: Environmental Ecology (`EE_Methodology.png`)
Its topics are heat stress and flash floods, the same two hazards as ours.
- **Columns, left to right, each with a one-word header:**
  - Focus: two hazard icons, heat stress and flash floods;
  - Analysis: three coloured boxes, solar radiation (yellow), outdoor thermal comfort (orange), water runoff (blue);
  - Simulation: 2021 / 2050 / 2100;
  - Risk areas detection: stacked layers, then a network of points;
  - Design;
  - Validation.
- **One feedback loop:** the "Optimization" arrow from Validation back to Design. One side input, "Catalogues of solutions", feeds Design.
- **Style:**
  - black background and a single sans font;
  - icons in white circles;
  - a colour that stands for each variable, kept the same in the result maps;
  - thin curved grey connectors;
  - no sentences, about 25 words in the whole figure.

## The others
- **Urban Morphology:** a sunburst. The inner ring holds the phases (analysis → development → output / user interface), the outer rings their sub-steps and data, with icons at the edge.
- **Energy and Social Economy:** icon-only pipelines (layers → clusters → strategies) on a transparent background.

## What to copy
1. **Column headers as the method's phases**, read in one glance.
2. **One colour per variable,** carried into every result map: heat orange, runoff blue, canopy green.
3. **Icons and 1–3 words per node;** the explanation goes in the paragraph under the figure.
4. **Show only the one loop that matters** (optimise ↔ evaluate) and the one side input (the city's rules, in the place of their "catalogue").
5. **One overview figure plus a few small per-module figures,** in the same grammar.
