# Shade & Sponge: personal part of the RM final presentation (3 min)

Brief (RM final session): "Each of you · 3 minutes · Your research. Recap your research through the course: topic and context, state of the art, research gap, refined question, and your method, and why this way." It follows the 5-minute cluster pitch.

Six slides, about 30 s each. Speaker notes = the "Say" lines. Numbers: 10 Nov tutor deck, 16 Nov city-wide rerun, 17 Nov back-test (26 replacement groups).

### 1. Topic and context
**Say (63 words):** "My thesis is Shade and Sponge. The context: Barcelona replaces street trees one at a time, when a tree dies or a street is rebuilt. Since 2018, almost eleven thousand trees got a new species. Plane trees, Platanus, are the largest single case. The city's master plan fixes how much of each species, at most fifteen percent, but not which species goes where."

### 2. State of the art
**Say (49 words):** "What exists today. Workflows that model heat and runoff together, but for fixed scenarios, with no choice of species. Optimisers that place trees, but for heat only. Rankings of species by their services, with no positions. And SylvCiT, a trait-based species recommender whose runoff module is still switched off."

### 3. Research gap
**Say (55 words):** "So my gap is methodological. Of eighteen tools that place or assess urban trees, none chooses the species tree by tree inside a city's own rules. And that choice matters. Here are trees with the same trunk size on Porta's streets: their crowns go from a full plane tree down to a quarter of one."

### 4. Refined question
**Say (78 words):** "My question changed a lot during this course. It started as trees against floods in Bologna. Your feedback narrowed it: runoff depends more on the tree pit than on the species, so runoff is now a check, not a promise. And the tool is for any tree, not only planes. So today: when a project must replace trees, can a trait-based method, inside the city's rules, do better than the city's own practice, or at least no worse?"

### 5. Method
**Say (63 words):** "The method reads open data: the tree inventory, LiDAR crowns, terrain, sidewalks, a hot day and a design storm. You draw a project area. For every tree the project removes, and every free spot, it scores each species on heat, runoff and canopy, and picks the best layout under the city's rules. Then it compares it with today and with the city's practice."

### 6. Why this way
**Say (71 words):** "Why this way. An exact optimiser, because the city's rules are hard limits: the answer is the best layout, and it is reproducible. Open data only, so it transfers: Mataró and Florence are next. A baseline that is what the city really plants. And checks: heat with Ladybug and Infrared City, and a back-test on twenty-six real replacement groups, where the method gives more than twice the summer shade per tree."

Total: 379 words (about 2 min 50 s).
