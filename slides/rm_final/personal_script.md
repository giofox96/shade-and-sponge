# Shade & Sponge: personal part of the RM final presentation (mini Pecha Kucha, 3 min)

Brief (RM final session): "Each of you · 3 minutes · Your research. Recap your research through the course: topic and context, state of the art, research gap, refined question, and your method, and why this way." It follows the 5-minute cluster pitch.

v2 (18 Nov): rebuilt on the decisions of 18 Nov after the 17 Nov tutor session (`notes/tutor_feedback_17nov.md`): the designer narrative, four actions per tree, palette of 30 species, baseline called "City practice" (what the city really planted, a practice, not a rule), Porta in absolute numbers. Nine slides × 20 s. Speaker notes = the "Say" lines.

### 1. Who and what
**Say (51 words):** "I am a designer redesigning a street in Barcelona. The street layout is already decided. I have to assess the trees that stand there and decide which to keep, move or replace, and where to add new ones, so that the street is cooler in summer, and not worse for flooding."

### 2. Context
**Say (51 words):** "This happens all the time. Since 2018, almost eleven thousand street trees got a new species, one at a time; plane trees are the largest single case. The city caps every species at fifteen percent. My site, Porta, has twenty percent canopy: eight hectares short of the city's thirty percent target."

### 3. State of the art
**Say (48 words):** "What exists today. Workflows that model heat and runoff together, but for fixed scenarios, with no choice of species. Optimisers that place trees, but for heat only. Rankings of species by their services, with no positions. And SylvCiT, a trait-based recommender whose runoff module is still switched off."

### 4. Research gap
**Say (53 words):** "So my gap is methodological. Of eighteen tools that place or assess urban trees, none chooses the species tree by tree inside a city's own rules. And the choice matters: with the same trunk size, the species on Porta's streets carry crowns from a full plane tree down to a quarter of one."

### 5. Refined question
**Say (54 words):** "My question changed a lot in this course. It started as trees against floods in Bologna. Feedback narrowed it: runoff depends more on the tree pit than on the species, so it is now a do-no-harm check. Today: can a method beat the city's own practice on heat, and never be worse on runoff?"

### 6. Fixed and free
**Say (53 words):** "What is fixed: the street geometry, and the city's rules: the fifteen percent cap, sidewalk width deciding tree size, distances to lamps and signs. What is free: four actions per tree. Keep, move, replace, or add on a free spot. Removing without replacing is not an option. The designer decides; the tool advises."

### 7. Method
**Say (52 words):** "The method reads open data: the tree inventory, LiDAR crowns, terrain, sidewalks, a hot day and a design storm. For every position, it scores thirty species from the city's own catalogue on summer shade, winter sun and water held before a flood spot. Then it picks the best layout within the rules."

### 8. Why this way
**Say (57 words):** "Why this way. An exact optimiser, because the rules are hard limits: it finds the best layout and proves it. Open data, so it works in any neighbourhood. And a real baseline: what the city actually planted, not what it announced. That also questions the rules: one in five new plantings is larger than its sidewalk allows."

### 9. First evidence
**Say (52 words):** "First evidence. On twenty-six real replacement groups, at the same positions, the method gives a median of one hundred thirty-nine square-metre hours of summer shade per tree; the city's choice gave fifty-nine. It wins in twenty-two of twenty-six. Next: heat checked in Ladybug and Infrared City, and transfer to Mataró and Florence."

Total: 471 words (about 3 min at 155 words a minute).
