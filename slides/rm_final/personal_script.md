### 1. Who and what
**Say (51 words):** "Imagine you are a landscape designer who has to redesign a street in Barcelona. The layout is already decided, so you have to choose which trees to keep, move or replace, and where to add new ones, so that the street is cooler in summer without getting worse when it rains."

### 2. Context
**Say (67 words):** "This happens all the time: since 2018, almost eleven thousand street trees have been replaced with a different species, one at a time. The city caps each species at fifteen percent, but it says nothing about which species goes where. Yet the choice matters, because in Porta, my site, trees with the same trunk carry crowns from a full plane tree down to a quarter of one."

### 3. State of the art and gap
**Say (53 words):** "The pieces already exist, but separately. Some models assess heat and runoff together, but without choosing species, while other tools choose species but look at one service only, or ignore positions. Of the eighteen tools I reviewed, none picks the species tree by tree inside a city's rules, and that is my gap."

### 4. Refined question
**Say (61 words):** "I started from trees against floods in Bologna. Feedback narrowed it, because runoff depends more on the tree pit than on the species, so runoff became a do-no-harm check. Today the question is: to what extent can a method that chooses species and positions inside the city's rules give more summer cooling than the city's own practice, without losing rain interception?"

### 5. Fixed and free
**Say (50 words):** "The street layout and the city's rules are fixed, such as the fifteen percent cap and the sidewalk width that sets the tree size. What is free are four actions for each tree: keep, move, replace, or add, but never remove without replacing. The tool advises, while the designer decides."

### 6. Method
**Say (59 words):** "First, a screening runs once for every tree and free spot: it records what stands there, where it is, and what each of thirty species would do there in summer shade, winter shade and rain held. Then an exact optimiser picks the best combination within the rules, and I compare the result with today and with the city's practice."

### 7. Why this way, and not another
**Say (71 words):** "I ruled out field measurement, because a layout that does not exist yet cannot be measured, and machine learning, because there is no outcome data to train on. An exact optimiser fits because the rules are hard limits, and it proves its layout is the best. The baseline is what the city actually planted, which also shows that one in five of its new trees is too big for its sidewalk."

### 8. First evidence
**Say (69 words):** "As a first test, I took twenty-six groups the city really replaced, at the same positions. With the city's choice each tree gave a median of fifty-nine square-metre hours of summer shade, while with the method it gave one hundred thirty-nine, and the method won in twenty-two groups out of twenty-six. Next, I will check heat with Ladybug and Infrared City, and test the method in Mataró and Florence."

**Say (2 words):** "Thank you."
