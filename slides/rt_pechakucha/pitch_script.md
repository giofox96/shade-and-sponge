# Shade & Sponge: Pecha Kucha pitch (RT final assignment, due 11 Nov)

Master copy: Google Slides https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1O8O2D067r3mQlhJclCj3zMWG--tIN833bFw879TQBUw/edit (speaker notes = the text below).

Format: 15 slides x 20 s. Brief: RT_L05 slide 90. v2 (4 Nov evening) after checking against the lecture's common mistakes: context merged, figures simplified to big numbers, web app + limits added, close = an ask.

Sources: numbers from the 10 Nov deck (v7_02_context, v6_04_crowns, res_04_city_map, res_06_heat_check) and the RT A2 Theory of Change. Press figures marked as press.

### 1. Hook: which tree goes back?
**Say (44 words):** "Barcelona is about to replace thousands of plane trees. Every one of them leaves a gap in the shade of a street, and in the way that street holds back rain. My research asks a simple question: which tree should go back, and where?"

### 2. Context: the decision is taken (−56% planes, 15% cap, Porta 20% → 30%)
**Say (43 words):** "Barcelona has decided. Plane trees are about a quarter of its street trees and must drop to twelve percent, under a cap of fifteen percent per species. In Porta, a hot and flood-prone neighbourhood, canopy is twenty percent against a thirty percent target."

### 3. The gap (thesis statement)
**Say (42 words):** "Here is the gap. The master plan fixes quotas, how many of each species. It does not say which species goes where. And no rule, no city tool, measures what a choice does to shade or runoff. Thousands of small decisions, unmeasured."

### 4. Same rules, very different crowns + no tool does both
**Say (46 words):** "And the choice matters. Under the same rules and trunk size, the allowed species carry crowns from a full plane down to a quarter of one. Yet no tool chooses species for heat and runoff together. The closest one, SylvCiT, has its runoff module switched off."

### 5. Research question
**Say (45 words):** "So my question: can a trait-based method, working inside Barcelona's own rules, choose replacements and new spots that keep more cooling and hold back more rain than what the city actually plants? The baseline is not 'no trees'. It is the city's real replacement mix."

### 6. How it works (in → optimiser → out)
**Say (45 words):** "So I built Shade and Sponge. It reads open data: the tree inventory, LiDAR crowns, terrain, a design storm and a hot day. For every tree to replace, it proposes a species and a position, scored on shade and runoff together, inside the city's rules."

### 7. Web app (Porta, screenshot)
**Say (40 words):** "This is the web app. A designer picks a neighbourhood, sets priorities between summer shade, winter sun and runoff, and compares today, the city's rule and the optimised layout, tree by tree, in 3D. It is online and open source."

### 8. Innovation: rules inside, not after
**Say (43 words):** "Three things make it new. The city's rules are inside the optimiser, not checked afterwards: the fifteen percent cap, sidewalk width, a palette of thirty allowed species. It compares against the city's real practice. And it is site-agnostic: one data adapter per city."

### 9. First evidence (27/28, +9.2 pts, ≤0.2 °C)
**Say (43 words):** "First evidence. Across twenty-eight neighbourhoods, under the city-wide cap, the tool keeps more summer shade than the city's mix in twenty-seven, by a median of nine points. A Ladybug check shows pedestrian heat stays within two tenths of a degree of today's planes."

### 10. Limits, said upfront
**Say (44 words):** "And the limits, said upfront. Everything is simulated, not measured. The heat difference is small, and its sign depends on how porous the crowns are. Runoff differences are a few percent, not yet resolved. And the benefits arrive only when the new trees mature."

### 11. Actors
**Say (48 words):** "Who is involved? The city's parks department decides. Designers use the tool. Data comes from the Catalan mapping institute, the city and its water-cycle company. I test the heat side with Ladybug and am in contact with Infrared City. Florence's municipality has offered LiDAR for a transfer test."

### 12. Exploitation: what, how, for whom
**Say (45 words):** "What can be exploited? The method, through the thesis and a paper. The code and a public web app, open source, for designers and cities. A per-tree dataset of crowns and traits. And a transfer guide for any city that has to replace a monoculture."

### 13. Impact over time
**Say (47 words):** "Impact over time. Short term: an open, reproducible method with simulated gains across the city. Medium term: pilot streets with the parks department, and a transfer test in Florence and Mataro. Long term: canopy moving toward thirty percent, no species above fifteen, less runoff where streets flood."

### 14. Impact dimensions + TRL
**Say (50 words):** "It touches five dimensions, from comfort to policy: it turns a quota plan into a spatial one. By December I expect TRL four: validated in simulation, on real city data. A test with a real designer would bring it to five. A pilot street is the step after the thesis."

### 15. My ask: test on one real street
**Say (46 words):** "So here is my ask. The city will replace these trees anyway. I want to test this on one real street rebuild, with a designer or the parks department. That is the step from simulation to TRL five. Least loss, inside the city's rules. Thank you."
