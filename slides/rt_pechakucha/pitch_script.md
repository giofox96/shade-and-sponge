### 1. Hook: when a project must cut trees, how to replace them?
**Say (52 words):** "This is Porta, in Barcelona. When a project has to cut street trees, what is the best way to replace them? My thesis is a method that chooses the new species and positions so that heat and storm water get better, or at least not worse. Plane trees are my first case."

### 2. Context: −56% planes, 15% cap, 10,976 replacements since 2018
**Say (43 words):** "Barcelona replaces street trees one at a time, when a tree dies or a street is rebuilt. Since 2018 almost eleven thousand trees got a new species. Now the planes must drop from twenty-seven to twelve percent, and no species may exceed fifteen."

### 3. The problem: a qualitative rule, nothing measured
**Say (38 words):** "Here is the problem. A designer who must replace trees has only a qualitative rule, which practice mostly does not follow, and nothing that says whether the street ends up hotter or wetter. Thousands of small decisions, unmeasured."

### 4. Same rules, very different crowns; no tool works tree by tree inside a city's rules
**Say (43 words):** "And the choice matters. At the same trunk size, the allowed species carry crowns from a full plane down to a quarter of one. Of eighteen tools that place or assess urban trees, none chooses species tree by tree inside a city's rules."

### 5. Research question
**Say (42 words):** "So my question: when a project must replace trees, can a trait-based method, inside the city's rules, choose species and positions so that cooling and rain interception improve, or at least do not get worse, compared with what the city actually plants?"

### 6. How it works (in → optimiser → out)
**Say (45 words):** "So I built Shade and Sponge. It reads open data: the tree inventory, LiDAR crowns, terrain, a design storm and a hot day. For every tree to replace, it proposes a species and a position, scored on shade and runoff together, inside the city's rules."

### 7. Web app (Porta, screenshot)
**Say (40 words):** "This is the web app. A designer picks a neighbourhood, sets priorities between summer shade, winter sun and runoff, and compares today, the city's rule and the optimised layout, tree by tree, in 3D. It is online and open source."

### 8. Innovation: rules inside, real baseline, any tree
**Say (46 words):** "Three things make it new. The city's rules are inside the optimiser, not checked afterwards: the fifteen percent cap, sidewalk width and the city's species list. The baseline is what the city really plants, from its own inventory. And it works for any tree, any city."

### 9. First evidence (27/28, +9.2 pts, ≤0.2 °C)
**Say (52 words):** "First evidence. Across twenty-eight neighbourhoods, under the city-wide cap, the tool keeps more summer shade than the city's announced mix in twenty-seven, by a median of nine points. Against what the city actually plants, none of thirty-three neighbourhoods loses. And pedestrian heat stays within two tenths of a degree of today's planes."

### 10. Limits, said upfront
**Say (48 words):** "And the limits, said upfront. Everything is simulated, not measured. The heat difference is small, and its sign depends on how porous the crowns are. Runoff is a check: the differences stay below what the model can resolve. And the benefits arrive only when the new trees mature."

### 11. Actors
**Say (48 words):** "Who is involved? The city's parks department decides. Designers use the tool. Data comes from the Catalan mapping institute, the city and its water-cycle company. I test the heat side with Ladybug and am in contact with Infrared City. Florence's municipality has offered LiDAR for a transfer test."

### 12. Exploitation: what, how, for whom
**Say (45 words):** "What can be exploited? The method, through the thesis and a paper. The code and a public web app, open source, for designers and cities. A per-tree dataset of crowns and traits. And a transfer guide for any city that has to replace a monoculture."

### 13. Impact over time
**Say (52 words):** "Impact over time. Short term: an open, reproducible method with simulated gains across the city. Medium term: pilot streets with the parks department, and a transfer test in Florence and Mataro. Long term: canopy moving toward thirty percent, no species above fifteen, and no street left hotter or wetter by a replacement."

### 14. Impact dimensions + TRL (3 now → 4 by Dec)
**Say (54 words):** "It touches five dimensions, from comfort to policy: it turns a quota plan into a spatial one. Today it is at TRL three; by December, TRL four: validated in simulation, on real city data. A test with a real designer would bring it to five. A pilot street is the step after the thesis."

### 15. My ask: test on one real street
**Say (48 words):** "So here is my ask. The city will replace trees anyway, street by street. I want to test this on one real street rebuild, with a designer or the parks department. That is the step from simulation to TRL five. Least loss, inside the city's rules. Thank you."
