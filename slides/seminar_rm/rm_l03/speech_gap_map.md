# RM – my gap map ("Opening the boxes · Shade and Sponge"), about 90 seconds

Read it top to bottom, pointing at the boxes. Words in brackets are where to point.

## Script

> This map follows one street tree in Barcelona, from our cluster topic, data-driven decision making, down to my research question. [top]
>
> On the left, the data. A street too hot to walk and water on the street after a storm can now be mapped street by street, as UTCI and millimetres of runoff, from LiDAR and design storms. On the right, the city's rules: no species above 15%, and the sidewalk width sets the tree size. [left, right]
>
> In the middle, why anything changes. The city plan wants fewer plane trees: one species means one pest can hit everything, the plane has its own pests and drops its leaves in drought, and the plan sets biodiversity and climate targets. So the plane is the only species over the 15% cap, and it has to be replaced. But each replacement changes shade and runoff, and nobody measures it. [centre, problem]
>
> Two insights come from the sides. The data exists, but nobody links it to the species in each pit. And the rules fix how much of each species, not which species where. [insights]
>
> In the literature, the heat and runoff workflows model both services, but in fixed scenarios, without choosing species. The species tools choose species, but never for heat and runoff together. My idea combines the two: replace each plane with the species, allowed by the rules, that loses the least shade and rain interception. [amber boxes, idea]
>
> The funnel on the left checks it: of 18 tools that place or assess urban trees, 7 cover heat and runoff, 1 also picks species, and 0 work per tree inside a city's rules. So my gap is methodological: no method picks species per tree position on heat and runoff together, inside a city's replacement rules. [funnel, gap]
>
> My question: can a trait-based layout, inside Barcelona's rules, keep more cooling and rain interception than the city's own replacement? H1 asks if it is better on both, H2 if the best for heat and the best for runoff differ, and the limit is that the gain shrinks as storms grow. [RQ, H1, H2, limit]
>
> The dotted lines are what goes back to the city: a number for each choice it cannot see now, the city's rule-following replacement as the baseline to beat, a test of what the 15% cap costs, and honest gains, small and measured. And on the right, where else: 33 Barcelona neighbourhoods, then Mataró and Florence. [dotted loops, where else]

## If short on time (about 45 s)
Keep paragraphs 1, 3, 6 and 7.

## Possible questions
- "Why only 18 tools?" It is my prior-art matrix, not a systematic review; the count can grow as I read.
- "Isn't the gain too small?" That is expected: the aim is to replace with the least loss, inside the rules, and to measure it.
