# RM – common ground with Elena and David (about 90 seconds)

Sources: the cluster map, `notes/rm_cluster3_common_points.md`, `slides/seminar_rm/cluster3_speech.md`. Nothing new about their theses: if they changed something, ask them.

## Script

> Our three theses look very different. Elena works on how one neurodivergent person will feel in a place, from a photo. David works on native plants and water to make walking more comfortable in Colombia. I work on which tree replaces which tree on a street in Barcelona. When I put my literature and Elena's on one map, they don't overlap. So what we share is not a topic but a way of deciding.
>
> First, all three of us start from a coarse rule. The city decides with averages: a demographic label for a person, a fixed table for a tree. Each of us tries to predict the single case instead: this person, this place, this tree position.
>
> Second, we all have to predict before anything exists, through a stand-in. Elena uses a photo, David and I use simulation, and David and I share the same comfort index, UTCI. Heat is the one stressor in all three: Elena lists heat on the skin as something a photo cannot show, and David and I try to compute it.
>
> Third, every line starts from something people live and ends in a number. For Elena, a place felt with all the senses becomes one photo rating. For David, walking comfort becomes one UTCI value per scenario. For me, a street too hot to walk and water on the street become UTCI, millimetres of runoff, crown size and leaf area. The number decides, so what it leaves out matters. That is our shared risk: whoever decides sees only the number, so what is left out?
>
> And fourth, we test the same way. A model is only useful if it beats the simple answer: for Elena, a group average; for David, the existing conditions; for me, the city's own replacement rule. If the simple rule wins, that is still a result.
>
> So our shared contribution to data-driven decision making is this: options for the single case, with their limits stated, instead of one answer for the average.
