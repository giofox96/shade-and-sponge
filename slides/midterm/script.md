# Shade and Sponge: midterm Pecha Kucha (24 Nov 2026, 5 min)

15 slides × 20 s. Built on the RM 3-minute deck (`slides/rm_final/`) and the 17 Nov tutor feedback (`notes/tutor_feedback_17nov.md`): narrative first, then a method anyone can follow, Porta in absolute numbers, the four actions inside the method, runoff explained with litres. Speaker notes = the "Say" lines. About 47 words per slide, 741 in total: about 4.9 minutes at 150 words a minute, leaving room for pauses (RT feedback, 23 Nov: keep to time).

### 1. Title
**Say (48 words):** "Hi, my project is called Shade and Sponge, an urban street-tree recommender. Imagine a Barcelona street whose layout is already decided: what is left are the trees, which to keep, move, replace or add, so that the street is cooler in summer and no worse when it rains."

### 2. Context
**Say (51 words):** "Since 2018, Barcelona has replaced almost eleven thousand street trees, capping every species at fifteen percent but never saying which species goes where. I compare everything with the plane, the tree most often replaced: with the same trunk, one species matches its crown, while another makes only a quarter of it."

### 3. Porta in numbers
**Say (56 words):** "I chose Porta because heat, flood hazard and planes are all high there, with 832 planes and a surface 1.4 degrees hotter in summer. Eight hectares of crown are missing to the city's thirty percent target, while every free spot would add only a third of a hectare: what matters is the species in each spot."

### 4. State of the art and gap
**Say (49 words):** "Research covers each part of this problem, but separately: some models measure heat and runoff without choosing species, while other tools choose species for a single benefit. Of the eighteen tools I reviewed, none chooses the species tree by tree, for heat and runoff, inside a city's own rules."

### 5. Question
**Say (42 words):** "In October I started from floods, but runoff depends more on the tree pit than on the species, so I now ask how much more summer cooling a method can give than the city's own practice, while runoff becomes a do-no-harm check."

### 6. Fixed and free
**Say (43 words):** "The project fixes the street, while the city fixes the fifteen percent cap and the sidewalk width, which sets the tree size. What stays free are four actions per tree, keep, move, replace or add, and the tool advises, while the designer decides."

### 7. Method
**Say (48 words):** "Heat and floods decide the data, from LiDAR to storms and sidewalks. Every tree is screened, the designer sets an action for each one, and an exact optimiser picks the species within the city's rules. Finally, I compare the result with today's trees and with the city's practice."

### 8. Screening
**Say (47 words):** "The screening fills one row for every tree and free spot, saying what stands there, from species to leaf density, and where, from the sidewalk to whether rain flows to a flood spot; each row then scores all thirty species, and the optimiser picks one per row."

### 9. City rules
**Say (43 words):** "The rules come from the city's street-tree guide, which allows no tree on sidewalks under two and a half metres and, above that, lets the width decide between small, medium or large trees. With the cap, these are rules the optimiser never breaks."

### 10. Where new trees go
**Say (45 words):** "Of Porta's two hundred sixty-seven possible spots, one hundred fifty-three are free and one hundred three conditional, because a sign, a crossing, a lamp or a bollard is in the way, which a project can move. Only eleven are blocked, by traffic lights and driveways."

### 11. A crown is a bucket
**Say (51 words):** "One millimetre of rain on one square metre is one litre: in a two-year storm about a thousand litres fall on a typical crown here, and the leaves hold about one hundred twenty-five. So, where rain heads to a flood hotspot, the method puts the species that hold the most water."

### 12. The tree on the hill
**Say (58 words):** "But on this terrain, rain on three positions in four never reaches a hotspot, so there the litres held change nothing; only a tree uphill of one keeps water out of it. Placed by the method, trees keep 5.6 times more than at random, yet only half a percent of the hotspot water, so runoff stays a check."

### 13. City practice, not a rule
**Say (48 words):** "My baseline is what the city really planted: a practice, not a rule, since one new tree in five is too big for its sidewalk. Since the method always follows the rules, I can switch one rule off and re-optimise, to measure what that rule costs in shade."

### 14. First evidence and co-benefits
**Say (47 words):** "On twenty-six groups the city replaced, the method gives one hundred thirty-nine square-metre hours of shade per tree against fifty-nine, winning twenty-two. In Porta it also stores more carbon than city practice and brings less pollen than today, while more diversity costs only 0.3 points of shade."

### 15. Next
**Say (55 words):** "Three steps come next: felt temperature, with Ladybug and Infrared City, to check that more shade means a cooler street; the transfer tests, where Mataró already gives eighty-three against sixty-three percent of summer shade, now with real LiDAR crowns, and then Florence; and the utility plans, to see which spots are really free. Thank you."
