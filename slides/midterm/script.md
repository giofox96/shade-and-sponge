# Shade and Sponge: midterm Pecha Kucha (24 Nov 2026, 5 min)

15 slides × 20 s. Built on the RM 3-minute deck (`slides/rm_final/`) and the 17 Nov tutor feedback (`notes/tutor_feedback_17nov.md`): narrative first, then a method anyone can follow, Porta in absolute numbers, the four actions inside the method, runoff explained with litres. Speaker notes = the "Say" lines. About 50 words per slide (150 words a minute).

### 1. Title
**Say (52 words):** "Imagine you are a landscape designer who has to redesign a street in Barcelona. The street layout is already decided. Your job is the trees: which to keep, which to move or replace, and where to add new ones, so the street is cooler in summer and not worse when it rains."

### 2. Context
**Say (59 words):** "This is not rare. Since 2018 Barcelona has replaced almost eleven thousand street trees with a different species, often one at a time. The city caps each species at fifteen percent, but says nothing about which species goes where. And the choice matters: with the same trunk, crowns range from a full plane tree to a quarter of one."

### 3. Porta in numbers
**Say (55 words):** "My site is Porta, in Nou Barris: eighty-four hectares, almost three thousand street trees. Tree crowns cover seventeen hectares, twenty percent. The city's target is thirty, so eight hectares are missing. Planting every free spot on its sidewalks adds about a third of a hectare. So what counts is which species stands in each spot."

### 4. State of the art and gap
**Say (52 words):** "The pieces exist, but separately. Some models assess heat and runoff together, without choosing species. Other tools choose species, but for one service only, or without positions. Of eighteen tools I reviewed, none chooses the species tree by tree, on heat and runoff, inside a city's own rules. That is my gap."

### 5. Question
**Say (50 words):** "So my question is: when a street project must replace or add trees, how much more summer cooling can a method give, choosing species and positions inside the city's rules, than the city's own practice, without losing rain interception? Runoff is a do-no-harm check, because there species matter much less."

### 6. Fixed and free
**Say (54 words):** "I declare what is fixed: the street layout, the fifteen percent cap, the sidewalk width that sets the tree size, traffic lights and driveways. What is free are four actions per tree: keep, move, replace, or add. Never remove without replacing. The tool advises; the designer decides, because only they see the trees' condition."

### 7. Method
**Say (57 words):** "The method has four steps. First, a screening of every tree and free spot. Second, the designer sets an action for each tree, with a reason: dead, diseased, dangerous, roots, works. Third, an exact optimiser picks the species for every replaced or added position, within the rules. Fourth, I compare with today and with the city's practice."

### 8. Screening
**Say (53 words):** "The screening asks three questions everywhere. What stands there: species, crown size and leaf density, from the city inventory and LiDAR. Where it is: sidewalk width, nearby objects, and whether rain from here flows to a flood spot. And what each of thirty species would do there: summer shade, winter shade, rain held."

### 9. City rules
**Say (54 words):** "The rules come from the city's street-tree guide. Below two and a half metres of sidewalk, no tree. Then small trees every seven metres, medium every nine, large every thirteen, set by the width. Plus distances from lamps and signs, and the fifteen percent cap per species. The optimiser cannot break any of them."

### 10. Where new trees go
**Say (53 words):** "Where can new trees go? Porta has two hundred sixty-seven candidate spots. One hundred fifty-three are free. One hundred three are conditional: a sign, a crossing, a lamp or a bollard is in the way, which a project can move after a field check. Only eleven are blocked, by traffic lights and driveways."

### 11. A crown is a bucket
**Say (51 words):** "Now rain, in litres. One millimetre on one square metre is one litre. In a two-year storm, about a thousand litres fall on a typical crown in Porta. The leaves hold about one hundred twenty-five; the rest reaches the ground. Wherever you plant it, the same tree holds the same water."

### 12. The tree on the hill
**Say (60 words):** "But position decides where that water would have gone. A tree upslope of a flood spot keeps its litres out of it. Three quarters of Porta's positions drain to no flood spot. Placed by the method, trees keep more than five times more water from flood spots than at random, but still under one percent. So runoff stays a check."

### 13. City practice, not a rule
**Say (54 words):** "My baseline is the city's practice: what it really planted where trees were replaced. It is a practice, not a rule, and it does not always follow the rules: one in five new trees is larger than its sidewalk allows. So the method can also measure what each rule costs, and inform updating them."

### 14. First evidence
**Say (52 words):** "First test: twenty-six groups where the city really replaced trees, same positions. With the city's choice, each new tree gives a median fifty-nine square-metre hours of summer shade; with the method, one hundred thirty-nine. The method wins in twenty-two of twenty-six groups, and sends no more water to flood spots in any."

### 15. Next
**Say (51 words):** "Next: heat checked as felt temperature with Ladybug and Infrared City, the same method tested in Mataró and Florence, and a tool where the designer picks the action tree by tree and sees the trade-offs. Shade and Sponge: the right tree, in the right place, within the city's rules. Thank you."
