## Primary: one shared claim

**Cities decide with averages; both theses replace an average-based rule with a prediction for the individual case, and test whether it beats the rule.**

| | A · Shade and Sponge | B · SenCity |
|---|---|---|
| The average-based rule today | Master plan: how much of each species, not which species where; a qualitative district × sidewalk table (`notes/topic_decision.md` §9–10) | Maps, design data and sensory guides "show the average, not the person"; personas from demographic labels |
| The individual case | One plane-tree position in Porta | One person in one Vienna place |
| The prediction | Heat (UTCI) and runoff per species and position | That person's comfort and stressors from one photo |
| The baseline it must beat | S1, the city's own rule-following replacement | Label-only personas and simple baselines (H1 vs H2) |

## Secondary: shared axes (Elena's Miro themes, read by both)

| Theme | A | B | The link |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vulnerability** | Summer heat stress and pluvial runoff; a plane-dominated street stock (15% cap: pests, climate, biodiversity) | Sensory sensitivity: noise, glare, **heat**, crowds | **Heat is the one stressor in both.** B lists "heat on the skin" as lost in a photo; A computes it. | **Enable non-experts** | Planners without an ecologist's background; web front-end | The people themselves; planners | The lecturers' "shared method" (§7) |
| **Preference / choice** | The heat vs runoff weighting; who sets it | Avoid or seek sensations | "Whose preferences shape the design" (§7) |
| **Impact on people** | Cooler, drier streets | Know what to expect; more liveable spaces | Public space that works for more people |
| **Motivation** | Generic planting; one-species legacy | Left out of city life; one type of mind | The city designed for one case (one species, one mind) |

## Tertiary: the shared risk

Both theses name the same failure in their "World" stage: **confident advice that is wrong**. A: false precision from an uncalibrated LAI proxy. B: a confident rating that is wrong for the person who relies on it. A shared answer is to show uncertainty and state limits, which also answers the lecturers' "who notices when it fails?".
