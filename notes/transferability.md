# Transferability of the study and the tool

*Draft v2, 18 Oct 2026 (v1 corrected after a `fact-checker` pass: module claim, species-overlap sample sizes, status symbols, absence claims). For the 20 Oct talk (slide 10) and the discussion chapter. Sources: `databases/data_inventory_barcelona.md`, `databases/data_inventory.md`, `notes/topic_decision.md` §6.1, `notes/methodology.md` §4 and §8, `shade_sponge/README.md`, `databases/transfer_species_overlap.csv`.*

**Claim in one line:** the **code** is site-agnostic by construction, the **evidence is not**. The geometry and physics kernels carry no local constant (`topo.route`, `heat.sun` and the shadow geometry, `runoff.scs`), while every module that reads data does: `heat.py` holds Barcelona design days and hours, `runoff.py` loads the Barcelona-calibrated `runoff_params.json` and the PDISBA IDF table at import, and `site.py`, `season.py` and `layout.py` are local by design. So only `topo.py` is constant-free as a file; the transfer cost is almost entirely the cost of rebuilding the local data and re-deriving the local parameters. One dataset (a classified **leaf-on** point cloud) and one layer (a city **pluvial flood map**) are hard dependencies that no fallback replaces today.

Why this belongs in the talk: the method is calibrated in one 0.84 km² neighbourhood with 2,825 street trees (`notes/site_selection.md` §5). The tutor asks for a contribution to knowledge, so the thesis has to say where the method holds and what it costs to move it, rather than implying it is universal.

## 1. Four degrees of transfer
| | Case | New data needed | Cost | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T0** | Another neighbourhood of Barcelona (**Sant Antoni**, flood-dominant contrast) | None: the same inventory, LiDAR, IDF and flood model cover the whole city. Only the site adapter (`build_<name>`) and the hotspot extract change | days* | planned, methodology §8 |
| **T1** | Another Mediterranean city with mature open data (**Florence**, planned transfer case) | New adapter + 6 datasets; traits and phenology re-derived; the decision variable redefined (§5) | 2–3 weeks* | planned 2–10 Dec, **data access unchecked** (`notes/agent_backlog.md`) |
| **T2** | A city with no open point cloud, or no species-level inventory at all (**Bologna** is the inventoried fallback: it *has* an inventory, but no confirmed city LiDAR) | As T1, and where the inventory is missing too, the baseline goes with it. The **design front-end still works** (§6): the designer supplies the positions | weeks–months* | not planned |
| **T3** | Another climate (non-Csa) | Everything in T1, plus the interception constant, the leaf calendar, the storm season and the heat design days lose their anchors (§4) | out of scope | **not claimed** |

\* Order-of-magnitude estimates, not measured. They are also in tension with the plan: `notes/methodology.md` §10 allots 2–10 Dec (9 days) to Sant Antoni **and** Florence together. Reconcile before quoting them to the tutor.

The thesis validates T0 and attempts T1. T2 and T3 belong to the discussion, as limits, not results.

## 3. Three things that do not travel, in order of severity
1. **A classified, leaf-on point cloud (row 3).** The LAI proxy is `−ln(gap fraction)/k` computed from the classified points inside each segmented crown (`scripts/bcn_lidar_porta.py`), and LAI drives both objectives: canopy storage `S = s·LAI` for runoff and opacity `1 − exp(−0.5·LAI)` for shade. A 1 m DSM, which is what Tuscany publishes, yields height and crown width but no gap fraction, so on a DSM-only city the species differences collapse to crown size. **Fallback:** literature or database LAI per species, with the sensitivity range already used for the gap-filled species — i.e. the transfer city loses the one trait we measure ourselves.
2. **A city pluvial flood map (row 5).** Tested, not assumed: the tool's own routing (Priority-Flood on DTM + buildings, no sewer) **barely separates** RESCCUE wet from dry cells in Porta — ROC AUC 0.52 in all four scenarios, area-matched hit rate 25% vs 23% random at T10 present. Only the extreme tail is informative: of the top 0.5% of open ground by upstream area, 58% (T10 present) to 80% (T100 future) is wet, 1.7–2.6× the random rate (`scripts/compare_resccue_runoff.py`, methodology §4 validation iii). So hotspots must come from a municipal map; without one, the runoff objective reduces to total volume and the *placement* half of the method loses its target.
3. **Traits and phenology (rows 7, 8).** Transfer makes the trait gap **worse, not better**: measured-trait coverage falls from 40% of trees in Barcelona to 19% in Florence (7% with both a runoff and a heat value). The partial consolation, measured in `databases/transfer_species_overlap.csv`: 63.5% of Florence's top-30 trees (41,944 trees, 51% of the full inventory) are species that already grow in Porta with LiDAR crown, height and LAI medians, and 74.0% match at genus level. **Read that headline with the sample sizes:** four of the fifteen matches rest on a handful of Porta trees — *Cupressus sempervirens* (10.4% of Florence's top-30) on **2** trees, *Pinus pinea* (4.2%) on **1**, *Magnolia grandiflora* on 1, *Cercis siliquastrum* on 4 — and `shade_sponge/site.py` already treats a 5-tree Porta sample as too thin to use (`CITY_LIDAR`, issue #42). Requiring at least 5 Porta trees, the overlap falls to **43.5% of the top-30 (28,730 trees, 11 species)**; at 10 trees, 39.5%. So the defensible claim is that the trait table and the gap-filling classes carry over for **roughly 40–45% of Florence's top-30 stock, i.e. about a third of its inventory** — and even there Porta's crown medians reflect Barcelona's planting age and pruning, so the geometry must be re-measured from Florence's own survey. The largest species that match nothing in Porta are *Quercus ilex* (8.5% of the top-30) and *Olea europaea* (6.2%), both Mediterranean evergreens of the kind the coverage note flags, then *Quercus robur* 1.7%, *Carpinus betulus* 1.2% and *Aesculus hippocastanum* 1.0%, which are temperate deciduous. (Sanity check in the same file: Porta covers 97.9% of Barcelona's own city-wide top-30, 90.5% on samples of 5 trees or more — which is why the site is a good calibration case and a weak test of generality.)

## 4. Parameters that must be re-derived, not copied
| Parameter | Value and anchor | Why it is local |
|---|---|---|
| `s` = 1.75 mm per unit LAI | Calibrated on Anys & Weiler (2024): 16 urban *Tilia*/*Acer*, Freiburg, 51 events Apr–Sep, 6.7 mm mean event (`notes/decisions/interception_parameter_definition.md`) | **Already a transfer**: temperate climate (Cfb), temperate species, moderate events. Lower bound 0.86 mm (surface storage only, Xiao & McPherson 2016) is carried through the runoff-module sensitivity runs for that reason (it is not varied in the tool-v0 sensitivity, which sweeps `OP_BARE` and the phenology instead) |
| Storm months | 78% of intense storm days in May–Oct (Esbrí et al. 2026, Barcelona) | Decides how much the leaf calendar matters. A city with winter-dominated storms would weight leafless months and could change the species ranking |
| Leaf calendar `phenology_palette.csv`, `R_OFF` = 0.5 | January photo evidence for Barcelona; `R_OFF` an assumption (test 0.3–0.7) | Phenology shifts with latitude and winter temperature; the whole winter-sun trade-off rests on it |
| Heat design days 15 Jul / 15 Jan, hours 12–17 / 10–15 | ASSUMPTION until the EPW hottest/coldest weeks | Latitude and climate; the sun model itself takes `(lat, lon)` and needs no change |
| `HAZARD_HIGH` = 40 | Resilience Atlas flood-hazard index, scale 5–50 | A threshold on **a Barcelona-specific index**. It has no meaning in another city's hazard product |
| `CAPTURE_M` = 100 m | ASSUMPTION, stand-in for sewer inlets (test 50–200 m) | Depends on the local drainage layout, which we do not have for any city |
| `OP_BARE` = 0.35, `k` = 0.5 | Both **ASSUMPTION** in the code: `OP_BARE` anchored second-hand on McPherson 1984 and Heisler 1982/84 via Thayer & Maeda 1985 (test 0.25–0.54); `k` = 0.5 still needs its own tag (`notes/methodology.md` §12.3) | Not site-specific, so they travel — but they travel as assumptions, not as measured values |

## 5. The question travels less well than the method
The thesis asks **which species replace Porta's plane trees**. That question exists because Barcelona's tree master plan caps every species at 15% while plane trees are ~29% of the street stock (D1) — a live municipal decision with a given palette and a given set of positions. No equivalent programme is recorded for Florence or Bologna — but neither inventory was searched for one, and `databases/data_inventory.md` S12 still lists the *Regolamento del verde* of both cities as 'to collect', which is where such a rule would sit. Treat this as unchecked, not as established absence. On transfer, the decision variable therefore has to be redefined: species at **renewal** positions (trees removed in the normal cycle), or species at **new** positions on plantable sidewalk cells, with a diversity constraint instead of a named palette. The optimiser does not change (positions × feasible species, two objectives, a share cap); what changes is who supplies the candidate positions and the cap. **This is worth saying explicitly in the talk**: it is a framing limit, not a code limit, and it is the kind of specificity the tutor asked for.

## 6. Two front-ends, two transfer profiles
`CLAUDE.md` already commits to one core and two front-ends, and they do not transfer equally:

- **Planning mode** (city-scale replacement, as in Porta) needs rows 1–9: inventory, point cloud, IDF, flood map, traits, phenology, policy. This is the demanding path and the one the thesis validates.
- **Design mode** (Grasshopper, the primary persona) needs far less: the designer supplies the site geometry and the candidate positions, so rows 2, 3 and 9 are replaced by the model on screen. What remains mandatory is an EPW, a terrain and building model, species traits, **a local IDF curve** (`runoff.design_storm` reads the PDISBA table) and **a leaf calendar and storm season** (`season.py`). **This is the honest answer to "is the tool transferable?"** — as a design tool, almost anywhere in the Csa belt; as a planning tool, only in cities with Barcelona-grade open data.

## 7. What can be claimed on 20 Oct, and what cannot
**Can:** the core is site-agnostic (a new site = `build_<name>()` + `trees_<name>()` with the same keys, `shade_sponge/README.md`); the geometry and physics kernels carry no local constant (`topo.route`, `heat.sun` and the shadow geometry, `runoff.scs`), while every module that reads data holds something local — `site.py` (adapter, `HAZARD_HIGH`, `CAPTURE_M`), `season.py` (calendar, storm months), `layout.py` (palette, cap), and also `heat.py` (design days and hours, `OP_BARE`) and `runoff.py` (the calibrated `s` and the PDISBA IDF path, both loaded at import); the data cost of a transfer is enumerated above; the Florence species overlap is measured.
**Cannot:** that the results transfer. No layout, Pareto front or species ranking from Porta has been tested anywhere else yet. Transfer is scheduled for 2–10 Dec and Florence data access is still unchecked, so the talk should present transferability as a **designed property of the tool plus a measured data bill**, not as a result.
