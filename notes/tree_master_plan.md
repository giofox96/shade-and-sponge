## 1. The rule as written

- **The 2037 target (P §2.1, pp. 24–25; M same text).** Target 3 of 5 for 2037: a biodiverse tree stock in which, within the urban fabric (*trama urbana*), *"cap espècie d'arbrat representi més del 15% del total"*. Target 2 sits next to it: 40% of species within the urban fabric adapted to global change, up from 30% today.
- **Its origin (P §1.4.4 box "Més diversitat d'espècies", pp. 20–21; M p. 17).** In 2012 the city started a project to balance street trees and palms, so that **within 50 years** no species exceeds 15% of the population. That is a horizon of about 2062, not 2037.
- **The older wording (G p. 5, p. 16, p. 21).** No species above 15% of the total, reached progressively. Selected species must stay below 15% of street trees. Management principle: a maximum of 15% of trees of the same species.
- **Scope = city-wide, over the urban fabric.** P's target is "within the urban fabric", which P §1.4.3 (pp. 18–19) counts as about 201,600 municipal trees, including street rows *and squares*. The 2012 project counts street trees and palms; G p. 16 says street trees. **No source applies the cap per neighbourhood or per street.** The repo's 140,404-tree street inventory is narrower than P's denominator.
- **The plan text does not contain "15% by 2027" or "12% by 2037".** Those dates come from the press (§4).

## 2. Every reason given for the cap

| # | Reason | Official source | Press |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pest or disease risk.** A stock dominated by one species can lose a large part of its trees to one pest or disease, so monospecific populations must be avoided | P §1.4.4; G p. 5, p. 16 (more diversity, less vulnerability to pests); G p. 17 (the plane's dominance put the city in a "position of vulnerability due to monoculture") | N2 (an eventual pest); N4, N5 (pests, diseases) |
| 2 | **Climate-change resilience.** A mixed stock copes better with climate effects | P §1.4.4; P objective 4 and target 2 (§2.1) | N5 (drought, heat, extreme weather) |
| 3 | **Biodiversity**, including the fauna that trees support | P target 3, objective 3; G p. 16 | N1 |
| 4 | **A diverse landscape with local identity** (each street or neighbourhood with its own species) | G p. 5, p. 21 | — |
| 5 | **Fewer disservices.** Diversify and avoid the most problematic species (allergies, VOC emissions, invasiveness, pavement damage, litter) | P §1.3.4, p. 14; G p. 16 (avoid thorny, allergenic or brittle species) | — |
| 6 | **Policy continuity since 1995.** 1995 green-space plan: balance the main species and replace poorly adapted ones. 1998 Eixample plan: the dominant plane had more sick and ageing trees | P §1.4.3; G p. 10 (1998 Eixample plan), p. 17 (replacement programme since 1995) | — |

**Reasons specific to the plane** (why it is the species being cut):

- Official: the only species above 15%, with "difficulty adapting" (G catalogue, p. 54). Main host of the plane lace bug *Corythucha ciliata* and of plane powdery mildew, plus bark canker (G p. 37). Loses its leaves suddenly in summer under water stress (G p. 38, box "L'estrès dels plàtans").
- Press only 🟡:
  - Allergies (N1 Calderón) and nuisance from the fruit (N2 Guitart; N4, N5, after Sant Jordi 2026). Guitart says the fruit is not allergenic and the pollen is (N2).
  - Heavy roots that lift the paving, intensive pruning, and crowns that reach balconies (N1 Calderón).
  - Less suited than 150 years ago: it rains less often but harder, it is warmer, and water does not infiltrate the street (N1, N2 Guitart).
- Background, not a Barcelona source: canker stain (*Ceratocystis platani*), the classic plane killer, was found in Catalonia in 2010 (Calonge, Girona) and declared eradicated in Feb 2014. EPPO lists Spain as "absent, pest eradicated" and France as "present, restricted distribution" (E).

## 3. Why 15 and not 10?

Read in full on 24 Oct: Santamour 1990 (#139), Kendal, Dobbs & Lohr 2014 (#140).

- **Barcelona gives no derivation.** P and G state the number without a citation or an analysis.
- **The number has a lineage in the literature.**
  - Barker (1975) proposed 5% per species to avoid repetitive landscapes. That was judged unachievable.
  - Later benchmarks were "mostly 10–15%": Grey & Deneke 1986, Santamour 1990, Miller & Miller 1991, Raupp et al. 2006 (as reviewed by Kendal et al. 2014, #140; the original sources were not read).
  - So Barcelona's 15% sits at the loose end of the established range.
  - The best-known version is Santamour's 10-20-30: no more than 10% of any species, 20% of any genus and 30% of any family (Santamour 1990, p. 64). Santamour himself says he does not know who first proposed the 10% rule (p. 58).
- **The rationale is the same as Barcelona's: protection from pests and diseases.** Santamour gives Dutch elm disease and chestnut blight as the cautionary cases (p. 58).
- **Santamour argues the species cap is the weakest of the three.** Pests follow genera and families, so a species rule "offers little protection"; diversity across genera matters most, and across families too (pp. 62–63).
  - **Barcelona caps species only.** It sets no genus or family limit.
  - Santamour names two pests and one family at risk that are relevant to our palette:
    - sycamore anthracnose on *Platanus* (p. 58);
    - fire blight, to which the rose family genera, including *Pyrus* (mostly *P. calleryana*), are "potentially susceptible" (p. 63);
    - the legume family, which includes *Sophora* (now *Styphnolobium*). He names no pest for *Sophora*; his legume example is mimosa webworm on *Gleditsia* and *Albizia*, and he notes "there may be limited similarity among genera in host-pest relationships" across the legume subfamilies (p. 63).
  - **Family check of the palette** (standard taxonomy, not from the plan):

    | Family | Palette species |
    |---|---|
    | Fabaceae (legumes) | Tipuana, *Styphnolobium* |
    | Rosaceae | *Pyrus* |
    | Cannabaceae | Celtis |
    | Meliaceae | Melia |
    | Bignoniaceae | Jacaranda |
    | Malvaceae | Brachychiton |
    | Platanaceae | *Platanus* |

    A family-level variant of the cap would therefore bind Tipuana and *Styphnolobium* together. This is an option for the tool, not a city rule.
- **The empirical check (Kendal et al. 2014, #140): 151 inventories from 108 cities.**
  - **Species level:** the most common species is far above 10% in every climate group. Santamour's 10% is close to the global 10th percentile.
  - **Genus and family:** the medians are close to 20% and 30%.
  - **Streets:** streetscapes are less diverse than parks (most common genus 24% vs 15%), and the authors suggest higher benchmarks for streets than for other land uses.
- **The warning that matters for this thesis.** Kendal et al. warn that targets set "substantially lower than existing relative abundance patterns" risk that "well adapted species may be abandoned for underperforming species (Richards, 1993)", or that "changing species may lead to unexpected changes in the provision of ecosystem services" (p. 415).
  - **It applies to Barcelona's planes,** at about 27% of street trees against a 15% cap and a 12% target.
  - **Context:** their sharper version, about "a reduction in the ecosystem service benefits" (p. 415), is written for streetscapes in Continental climates. Barcelona falls in their Temperate group, for which they say benchmarks may even need lowering (p. 416).
  - **Our inference, not Kendal's:** that the missing piece is performance data per species and position, which the method supplies.
- **15% is also the regional convention:**
  - a non-binding "reasonable 15%" in the Diputació de Barcelona's street-tree guide (2012, over a municipality's whole tree inventory);
  - the cap in L'Hospitalet's approved plan;
  - the objective in a Sabadell conference deck (not an approved plan).

  Girona is stricter (5-10-15). Details in `notes/catalan_transfer_city.md` §5.

**Health co-benefit of canopy, as context.** In Barcelona, only 4.7% of 3,145 surveyed residents met a surrogate of the 3-30-300 green-space rule. Surrounding greenness (NDVI ≥ 0.3 in 500 m) was associated with lower odds of poor mental health (OR 0.60, 95% CI 0.40–0.90) (Nieuwenhuijsen et al. 2022, #141). This is not a heat or runoff result. It only adds that losing crown area has costs beyond the two objectives of the tool.

## 5. Is it already known how to replace them?

**Partly.** The mechanism, the species mix and the placement rules are public. Which species goes on which street is not.

1. **Mechanism.** Gradual: planes are replaced as they die and as more trees are planted (N5), and removed during street works (N1). The big works are the lever, e.g. the Diagonal tram link and 22@ before it (N1, N2). The effort is concentrated in the Eixample and Sant Martí (N1, N2). The city frames this as "not eliminating them but integrating them in balance" (N5).
2. **Where planes stay.** On the large avenues and in emblematic places only. Since 1995 the planes elsewhere are being replaced progressively (G p. 17). The plane's identity value is explicit: the Eixample's main streets are associated with it (G p. 5). La Rambla replaced 23 declining planes with planes again, because its trees are catalogued as a set (Betevé, 19 Oct 2015 🟡: https://beteve.cat/societat/la-rambla-renova-i-amplia-el-nombre-de-platans/).
3. **Named replacements.**
   - Celtis (*lledoner*), Sophora (*Styphnolobium*), Melia and Tipuana "among others" (N1, N2). N1's *cínamom* is Melia (Spanish *cinamomo*, G p. 53), not cinnamon.
   - **Target mix (2026, N5):** planes and Celtis ~12%, Melia 7.25%, and about 5% each for *Pyrus calleryana*, Sophora and Tipuana. N5 does not say whether the 12% applies to each species or to both together. N4 and N5 put planes alone at 12% by 2037, so the reading "12% together" would leave Celtis at about 0%: "each" is the plausible one. Either way Celtis has to fall from its 13.8% (repo snapshot).
   - Melia rose from 3% to 4.37% (N4).
4. **Placement rules (official, G §9, pp. 22–23).** Tree size class by adult crown and height. Street class by **sidewalk width**:

   | Sidewalk | Tree class | Adult crown / height | Spacing | Min. distance to lamp |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | < 2.5 m | avoid planting | — | — | — |
   | < 3.5 m (narrow) | small | < 4 m / < 6 m | 7 m | 3 m |
   | 3.5–6 m (medium) | medium | 4–6 m / 6–15 m | 9 m | 4.5 m |
   | > 6 m (wide) | large | > 6 m / > 15 m | 13 m | 6.5 m |

   Also: no planting where the adult crown would come within 0.5 m of balconies or overhangs (stated under narrow streets). Keep > 3 m from traffic lights and signs, and > 1 m from driveways.
5. **Size class of each palette species (G catalogue, pp. 49–56; G/M/P = large/medium/small):**

   | Species | Size class | Note in the catalogue |
   |---|---|---|
   | *Platanus × acerifolia* | large | exceeds the 15%; difficulty adapting |
   | *Celtis australis* | large | close to the 15% |
   | *Tipuana tipu* | large | — |
   | *Jacaranda mimosifolia* | large | — |
   | *Melia azedarach* | medium | — |
   | *Styphnolobium japonicum* | medium | — |
   | *Brachychiton populneus* | medium, evergreen | — |
   | *Pyrus calleryana* 'Chanticleer' | small | — |

   PLATANOR® 'Vallis Clausa' is listed separately (p. 53), which bears on the backlog question of whether Porta's 3 trees of it count as *Platanus*. Each species also carries a coloured suitability mark (plant / restricted, i.e. only fill gaps in streets that keep the species / avoid / under study). **The colour cannot be read from the text extraction, so each species' mark is unverified.**
6. **Not public.** In 2022 the city said it had already decided which species can replace trees that die, using street orientation, available aerial space, soil quality and desired shade (N3 🟡). That list or rule set was not found. The plan has only the actions: 1.2 balance species abundance, 1.4 plan the renewal, 2.9 select species for the future (urban resistance, habit, flowering) (P §2.2). **Nobody publishes a per-street choice, and no source quantifies runoff or thermal comfort per species or position** (G p. 16 asks only qualitatively for shade trees that feel cool in summer). That is the gap the tool fills.

## 5b. Gate 1 (24 Oct): a structured "which species, where" rule exists, but on paper only

Tutor, 20 Oct: check whether Barcelona already has a structured plan, decided by a criterion, that sets which trees are replaced and where.

**Sources read in full text** (BCNROC, in the browser):
- **S** = *Arbrat viari: selecció d'espècies*, Parcs i Jardins, 2002, last revised May 2004, 56 pp. https://bcnroc.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/jspui/bitstream/11703/90277/1/3265.pdf. The table was read on the rendered page, not only from the text layer.
- **PG** = *Pla de gestió de l'arbrat viari de Barcelona*, Parcs i Jardins, Dec 2005, last revised Jan 2006. https://bcnroc.ajuntament.barcelona.cat/jspui/bitstream/11703/86305/4/3263.pdf.txt

**What they decide.**
1. **A rule for dead trees: one table, by district and sidewalk width** (S §2.4.2.1 p. 11; PG §3.4.3 p. 10, same table). In S it is marked "d'ús intern de Parcs i Jardins". **Row 8, Nou Barris:**

   | Sidewalk | < 3.5 m (small) | 3.5–6 m (medium) | > 6 m (large) |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Species to plant | *Prunus cerasifera* 'Atropurpurea' | *Melia azedarach* or *Jacaranda mimosifolia* | **keep *Platanus*** or the existing species; *Ulmus pumila* is replaced by a large species |

2. **Why the plane is replaced** (S §2.4.2; PG §3.4.2): it is kept only on large avenues, on exceptional streets with low, airy, sunny buildings, and where its growth does not interfere with urban activity. **Priority order for replacing a plane or elm:** (1) road-safety problems, (2) dead, (3) aesthetically unrecoverable or declining, (4) to unify a stretch where another species already dominates, (5) taking advantage of street works.
3. **Rebuilt streets** (S §2.4.2.2 p. 13): a broader list for each sidewalk class, with no district. It comes "from the experience of the technicians" and is revised periodically.
4. **Exceptions** (S §2.4.3): a street where conversion has already started with a different species keeps that species. A well-adapted small species on a narrow street is kept.
5. **Criteria named:** tolerance of local conditions, urban typology, tradition and earlier plantings (S §2.4.2). **Shade, heat and runoff are not among them.**

**The current guide drops the table.** The 2011 guide (G §11 p. 28), still in force under the 2017 plan, says only that a dead tree is replaced by the same species or a new one "taking into account the species catalogue". The catalogue's suitability marks include "restricted: only to fill gaps in streets that keep the species". No district table is printed. The 2022 press criteria (orientation, aerial space, soil, desired shade; N3 🟡) are newer than S/PG and are not in any document found.

**Does Porta follow the table?** Checked with `scripts/replacement_rule_check.py` → `databases/barcelona/replacement_rule_check.csv`. Inputs: Porta trees with a planting date (2007–2026, 559 of 2,825). Of these, 368 have a measured sidewalk class; 183 marked 'check' and 8 'none' are excluded.
- **Narrow sidewalks:** 58 planted; **10%** are the table's *Prunus cerasifera*. All six are recorded as 'Pissardii'; the match ignores the cultivar. Most are *Pyrus calleryana* (30).
- **Medium sidewalks:** 112 planted; **10%** match, all of them Melia (no Jacaranda). Brachychiton leads (26), then *Pyrus*, Handroanthus and Melia.
- **Wide sidewalks:** 198 planted. Only 11 are planes: 9 *P. × acerifolia* and 2 PLATANOR, whose status is disputed (§5.5). The other 187 are other taxa, 24 taxa (21 genera) in all: Grevillea 42, Casuarina 23, Fraxinus 23, Robinia 21, … The table also allows "the existing species", and the inventory cannot tell what each tree replaced.

**What the check cannot show.** The table covers dead trees only. Rebuilt streets use a broader list (S §2.4.2.2), and the exceptions apply (S §2.4.3). The inventory cannot separate these cases; the peaks of 70 plantings in 2010 and 102 in 2025 suggest street works. So ~90% is an **upper bound** on non-compliance. Planting dates exist for 20% of Porta's trees. The inventory does not record which species a new tree replaced. The size class comes from the 2021 façade-to-kerb measurement (`notes/sidewalk_width.md`).

**Verdict.**
- **Yes, a structured rule has existed since at least 2004.** It sets species by district × sidewalk width and replaces the plane everywhere except wide avenues.
- **It is qualitative.** It rests on tradition, typology and the technicians' experience; heat and runoff are not mentioned.
- **The 2011 guide no longer prints it.** Whether it is still used internally is unknown (question for the email).
- **It is not visible in Porta's plantings.** Up to ~90% of dated plantings on narrow and medium sidewalks do not match the dead-tree table. This is an upper bound, since rebuilt streets and exceptions follow other lists. The per-street list announced in 2022 is still unseen.

So the gap holds and becomes sharper: the city has qualitative rules for which species, but **no published rule that is visible in practice, and none that quantifies heat or runoff**. The email to Parcs i Jardins (sent 24 Oct) is the last check. If it returns a current per-street list, that list becomes S1.

## 6. Consequences for the thesis and the tool

1. **Cap scope.** `shade_sponge/layout.py` applies `MAX_SHARE = 0.15` **within Porta**, but the plan caps the **city-wide** street stock. Keep the local cap, but label it `ASSUMPTION` (a local reading of a city-wide rule). The three scopes in backlog #9 still stand, and scope (c), city-wide 12%, now has a 2026 source (press). A city-wide headroom must use P's urban-fabric denominator (~201,600 in 2017), not only the 140,404 street trees.
2. **Not every plane is a decision variable.** Planes on large avenues and in emblematic places are kept (G p. 17). Before fixing the 832 positions, check which of Porta's planes stand on avenues the city would keep.
3. **The façade-clearance constraint now has a sourced rule** (§5.4; backlog #9 said it had none). Sidewalk width sets the allowed size class, so a large species may only go on a > 6 m sidewalk. The palette's size classes (§5.5) make this a per-position filter. This needs sidewalk widths per position: extraction plan in `notes/sidewalk_width.md`.
4. **S1 baseline.** The 2026 target mix (§5.3) is the closest thing to "the city's named palette in its proportions". It is press-reported, so it is 🟡.
5. **Celtis has little room.** At 13.8% city-wide, it cannot absorb many replacements under any city-wide reading.
6. `notes/topic_decision.md` §7 says planes are "~29% of street trees". The latest figure is 27.45% (2026, N4). §7 is left as it was; v6 (§9, 21 Oct) uses the new figure and rebuilds the problem on this note.

## 8. City strategy documents, 2021 and 2024 (read 31 Oct)

PDFs and page-tagged text in `databases/policy/` (not committed). Page numbers are printed pages.

- **Both master-plan 2037 targets still stand in 2024.** C p.33 (adaptation goals): "Increase tree cover by 5% (2037)." and "Increase adapted tree species from 30% to 40%." The second matches P target 2. C does not say whether "5%" is points or relative and gives no baseline, so quote it as printed and do not convert it.
- **Tree pits are a drainage measure in C.** "Installing aligned tree pits." is listed under the goal "Reducing the risk of flooding due to poor drainage" (C p.43; D2 on p.62 reads "Installing more aligned tree pits."), next to permeable paving and 7 ha of SUDS (p.33).
- **City shade targets are structures, not trees.** 9 ha of shade "using either seasonal shading or fixed productive structures" (C p.33), the Shade programme of 216 shaded areas (p.60) and a Shade Plan (E5, p.65). No tree-based shade figure.
- **NP sets no street-tree or canopy target.** Its 2030 targets are in hectares and people (NP pp.9, 91: +160 ha of greenery from 2015, +100 ha naturalised, 10 biodiversity shelters, double the participants, 40 "All hands to greenery" projects). "Canopy" and "tree cover" do not occur. It names P only as a strategy (p.77: 50 actions in 10 lines) and gives no species list.
- **NP lists the missing knowledge as a study to do.** Project 83 (Action 14, p.114): study the plant species most adapted to shading and the areas that need especially thermoregulatory plants. The Greenery Model (p.38) prioritises, qualitatively, places where greenery can regulate the temperature of the urban fabric more; NP project 1 (p.98, lead project) plans the green infrastructure from that diagnosis.
- **Do not mix tree counts.** NP's 242,789 trees (2020, p.21) cover the urban fabric and parks, a different base from P's ~201,600 (urban fabric) and the repo's 140,404 street trees.
