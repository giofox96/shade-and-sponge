# Methodology (draft v1, 8 Oct 2026): Porta, Barcelona

**Method in one line:** a computational method that chooses **which species replace Porta's plane trees and where**, scoring every layout on **pedestrian heat stress (UTCI)** and **event runoff volume**, and returns the trade-off between the two (Pareto front).
Problem, research question and hypotheses: `notes/topic_decision.md` §7. Site: `notes/site_selection.md`.
Citations marked *(to add)* are standard references not yet in `papers/`/Zotero: add them before citing.

## 3. Tree model (shared by both modules)
For each tree: position, species, **height H, crown diameter D, crown base Hb** (existing trees from LiDAR, new trees from species size classes at planting and at maturity), **LAI** (leaf-on / leaf-off months), **canopy storage capacity S**, **leaf habit**, **wood anatomy** (diffuse- vs ring-porous, as a transpiration class).
- **Gap-filling:** species without measurements get genus or leaf-habit class values with a range. This currently covers Celtis, Melia, Tipuana, Jacaranda and Brachychiton (`databases/barcelona/species_coverage.csv`). Every result is re-run at the low and high ends of the range (sensitivity), and the missing data are reported as a finding.
- Canopy transmissivity for radiation: τ = exp(−k·LAI) (Beer–Lambert, Monsi & Saeki 1953 *(to add)*), k = 0.5 as in Peng et al. (2026) and in our LiDAR LAI proxy.
- **Species storage (option for runoff v2):** Xiao & McPherson (2016) give surface storage per unit leaf + stem area for 20 street species. Examples: *Platanus × hispanica* 0.87 mm, *Pyrus calleryana* 'Bradford' 0.51 mm, *Celtis sinensis* 0.71 mm (a congener of *C. australis*); mean 0.86 mm. A species factor s_sp = s · (C_sp / 0.86) would replace the single s; species without a value keep s and get a range. (Baptista et al. 2018 is not used as a capacity: their 15-min, 2.54 mm/h event delivers only 0.64 mm.)

## 4. Runoff module (Python, later a Grasshopper component via Hops)
Event based, per street segment. Policy hook: the city lists "Installing aligned tree pits." under its flood-risk goal, next to permeable paving and SUDS (Climate Plan 2024, p.43; `tree_master_plan.md` §8).
1. Rain depth P(T, d) from the PDISBA IDF curves, T = 1, 2, 10 yr, d = 1 h (plus observed storms if BCASA gauge data arrive; fallback: Netatmo personal weather stations, 5-min public data, r = 0.94 with official daily gauges for one event, 24 station pairs, Valencia, Rombeek et al. 2025; check station density in Nou Barris and the data terms first).
2. **Canopy interception** over each crown's projection on sealed ground: storage-bucket I = min(P, S) with S from species storage capacity × LAI. An analytical Gash-type model (Hassan et al. 2017; Huang et al. 2017) is the refinement; Huang et al. validated it on two conifers only, and found the evaporation-to-rain ratio the most sensitive input. Hassan et al. give Mediterranean parameters for one isolated tree each: *Quercus ilex* S 1.75 mm, p 0.31, c 0.69, E/R 0.38; *Q. pyrenaica* S 0.66 mm, p 0.74, c 0.26, E/R 0.21. They used wet-canopy evaporation from throughfall regression; Penman-Monteith underestimated interception by 48–60%. Stemflow is ignored (<1% of rain; Anys & Weiler 2024).
3. **Tree-pit infiltration**: pit area × infiltration rate × event duration (literature range; Bartens et al. 2008, Zhang et al. 2019). Absolute values from Bartens et al. (read in full): approximate container saturated conductivity of compacted clay loam (1.59 g/cm3) 1.3e-3 cm/s (~47 mm/h) without a tree, 3.0–3.9e-3 cm/s (~108–140 mm/h) with young oak or maple; 1.31e-3 vs 4.83e-5 cm/s (~47 vs ~1.7 mm/h) with vs without green ash (structural soil over compacted subsoil). These are lab values for the most restrictive layer, so use them only to bracket a sensitivity range. Zhang et al. (2019, read in full), three-year-old saplings in loam tanks, one year after planting: 0.28 m/d (~12 mm/h) without a tree, 0.33 m/d (+19%, fibrous *Malus baccata*), 0.61 m/d (~25 mm/h, +118%, tap-rooted *Sophora japonica* = *Styphnolobium*). Roots help; whether the effect depends on species is unclear (Bartens: oak vs maple not significant; Zhang: tap vs fibrous differ, young trees in tanks), so the term stays species-independent. Why the pit matters: once sealed surfaces drain onto the tree's soil, the canopy benefit fades and infiltration controls runoff; with run-on from 3× the soil area, raising infiltration from 0.036 to 3.6 mm/h cuts the November runoff probability from 89% to <1% (Marrazzo & Raimondi 2025, analytical model, *Platanus*, Lecco rain). Pit sizing (read in full): in Melbourne clay, 0.72 m² kerb pits draining ~200 m² kept a median 11% of runoff; ~90% retention needs a pit of 2.5–8% of its impervious catchment, and exfiltration (≥ 88%), not the young tree (~4%), removes the water (Grey et al. 2018). Established evergreen street trees transpired the equivalent of 17% of the annual runoff from the ~200 m² impervious catchment draining to each 6 m² trench, but the trench did not raise their transpiration (Thom et al. 2020; Apr–Sep modelled from ETo). If the pit is added, its variables are pit area relative to catchment and native-soil exfiltration; species enter through the crop factor (0.33–1.56 among 13 potted species, *Pyrus calleryana* in the high class; Thom et al. 2022), which matters little while trees are young (crop factor < 5% effect in Grey et al. 2018).
4. Remaining rain on sealed surfaces → SCS curve number (CN ≈ 98 sealed) → **event runoff volume (m³)**.
- **Validation:** (i) interception module vs the open Freiburg field data (Anys & Weiler 2024, FreiDok); (ii) per-tree and per-m²-canopy volumes vs paired-catchment benchmarks (Selbig et al. 2021: 6,376 L/tree; Coville et al. 2022: field 66 L/m² canopy per leaf-on season (Selbig et al., read in full: 4% of runoff, peak discharge unchanged; event data reported as a USGS data release, Carvin & Selbig 2021, not yet checked), calibrated i-Tree Hydro 6,120 L/tree and 63.5 L/m²; uncalibrated defaults gave about twice the total runoff); (iii) plausibility vs the flood-hazard index pattern. **Done 15 Oct against RESCCUE depth** (`scripts/compare_resccue_runoff.py`, open ground in Porta): upstream area from the no-sewer routing barely separates RESCCUE wet from dry cells (ROC AUC 0.52 in all four scenarios; area-matched hit rate 25% vs 23% random at T10 present), but the cells with the largest upstream area do fall in RESCCUE water: of the top 0.5% of open ground, 58% (T10 present) to 80% (T100) are wet, 1.7-2.6 times the random rate, falling to the random rate beyond about 10% (read from the curve in `notes/figures/resccue_vs_routing.png`). 44% of the atlas high-hazard cells used by the tool are wet at T10 present vs 23% of all open ground. Likely reasons, from the map and not yet tested: RESCCUE floods the perimeter avenues with water from outside Porta and from the sewer, which our routing lacks; our depression filling gives large upstream areas to plazas and the sports ground that RESCCUE leaves dry. Consequence: the routing locates the main convergence points but is not a flood map; hotspots should stay tied to the atlas index (or RESCCUE depth).

### 4b. Runoff module v1: implemented 8 Oct (`scripts/runoff_model.py`, calibration `scripts/calibrate_interception.py`)
- **Grid:** 1 m over Porta (83.7 ha): roofs 29% (OSM footprints, CN 98), sealed open space 52% (CN 98), pervious 19% (NDVI 2017 ≥ 0.3 and LiDAR canopy < 2 m, or OSM park/garden/grass; CN 74 = TR-55 open space HSG C, *assumption*).
- **Storms:** PDISBA city-level empirical IDF, design intensity (`databases/barcelona/pdisba_idf_city.csv`): T1-60 min 19.6 mm, T2-60 31.9 mm, T10-60 62.5 mm, T2-20 min 21.2 mm.
- **Trees:** 2,600 LiDAR street-tree crowns (disk of LiDAR crown diameter; LAI = LiDAR proxy). Non-street canopy (parks, squares, private; 10.2 ha) is fixed background (LAI 2.3).
- **Source of the LiDAR LAI proxy (checked 7 Nov):** `lai_proxy = −ln(gap)/k`, k = 0.5 (`scripts/bcn_lidar_porta.py`), is the gap-fraction inversion validated for an urban forest by Alonzo et al. (2015, Remote Sens. Environ. 162:141–153, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2015.02.025): LAIe = −ln(LPM)/(k·cos-correction), k = 0.5 for a spherical leaf angle distribution (fitted slope implied k ≈ 0.495), first + last returns incl. ground, then a clumping correction; vs hemispherical photos r² = 0.82, slope 0.99, RMSE 0.41 over 71 plots (Santa Barbara). Earlier forest precedent: Morsdorf et al. (2006, Remote Sens. Environ. 104:50–61, doi:10.1016/j.rse.2006.04.019), a first-echo share calibrated by regression on hemispherical photos (R² 0.69), G = 0.5. **Differences from Alonzo, to state as limits:** we invert per segmented crown, not per plot or 10 m pixel; our gap is the share of ground-class points among all points in the crown disk, not their return-type formula; no scan-angle or clumping correction, so ours is an effective LAI. Both papers are open PDFs (author pages); not yet in `papers/`.
- **Calibration** of effective canopy storage S = s·LAI on Anys & Weiler (2024) open field data (16 urban *Tilia cordata* / *Acer platanoides*, Apr–Sep 2021, 51 events ≥ 1 mm): **s = 1.75 mm per unit LAI** (pooled median; lime 1.85, maple 1.60), per-tree interception 38–78% of rain, event RMSE 0.9–3.2 mm. The effective value includes evaporation during events; for Barcelona's short intense storms it may be lower, so 0.86 mm (surface storage only, Xiao & McPherson 2016) is the lower bound in the sensitivity runs. Plausibility of s: leafy crowns hold the first 2–4 mm of a storm (Kuehler et al. 2017), and S = 1.75 × 2.3 ≈ 4.0 mm; Coville et al. (2022) set i-Tree Hydro's leaf storage depth to 2.0 mm (typical 0.07–0.6, mean 0.25 mm) from in-situ estimates to match the field data, while noting that the value may be site-specific. Lower-bound evidence: Leyton storage 3.5 mm at LAI 2.6 (birch) and 2.9 mm at LAI 4.3 (pine), i.e. ~1.35 and ~0.67 mm per unit LAI (Zabret & Sraj 2019; surface storage only, without evaporation during events); Jackson-method storage 1.75 mm at LAI 2.1 (*Quercus ilex*) and 0.66 mm at LAI 1.6 (*Q. pyrenaica*), i.e. ~0.83 and ~0.41 mm per unit LAI (Hassan et al. 2017). Default leaf storage of 0.2 mm (Wang et al. 2008 via Huang et al. 2017; Dickinson 1984 via Marrazzo & Raimondi 2025) may underestimate interception.
- **Results (T2-60):** current street trees reduce Porta runoff by **1.85%** (open space only: 2.9%); the range across s = 0.86–2.2 and T = 1–10 yr is 0.4–4.0% (open space 0.7–6.3%). Plausibility: the same order as the measured 3.5–4% (Selbig et al. 2021; Coville et al. 2022). City-wide, Cortinovis et al. (2022) also found a small effect for Barcelona: 20,170 new street trees raised runoff retention from 50.18% to 50.76% for a 20 mm event (curve number, trees as land cover). Replacing all plane trees by mature Jacaranda/Melia/Tipuana: +0.17/+0.22/+0.27% runoff; by Celtis/Brachychiton: +0.60%; by Pyrus: +0.69%; **any young replacement: ≈ +0.7% (≈ 37% of the current street-tree benefit lost during the transition)**. The benefit shrinks with return period, supporting H3. Figure: `notes/figures/runoff_results_v1.png`.
- **Sensitivity to s (7 Nov; `scripts/runoff_s_sensitivity.py` → `databases/barcelona/runoff_s_sensitivity_porta.csv`).** Porta, 7-species reference run, layouts held fixed, seasonal basis. Canopy interception of today's trees S0: 794 m³ at s = 1.75, 390 m³ at 0.86 (Xiao & McPherson 2016), 377 m³ at 0.83 (*Quercus ilex*, Hassan et al. 2017); the same at T = 2 and T = 10 yr (60 min), because the crowns fill in both storms. Every layout difference scales roughly with s and keeps its sign: best-summer S4 vs the city mix S1a, T = 2, −31.4 m³ total (−0.18%) and −9.9 m³ at the flood hotspots (−0.37%) at s = 1.75, vs −15.7 m³ (−0.09%) and −5.0 m³ (−0.18%) at s = 0.86; S4 vs today S0 stays within +0.13% total. So the ranking (S4 better than S1a, about level with S0) does not depend on s, but the runoff differences are well below 1% for any s in the evidence range: the runoff answer stays 'not resolved'.
- **Attempted cross-check of the proxy (18 Oct), inconclusive by construction:** Florence's *Life Escapos* table looked like per-tree measured LAI, but every value in it is a deterministic function of species and DBH (51 of 68 species flat; the other 17 an exact power law of DBH, log-log R² = 1.0000, exponent 0.12–0.25; `scripts/escapos_lai_check.py`). Across the 19 shared species the LAI ratio Escapos/proxy spans 0.69–2.07 and tracks the **crown-size difference between the two cities** rather than proxy accuracy: *Tilia × europaea* 2.07 at a crown ratio of 2.02, *Platanus × acerifolia* 1.09 at a crown ratio of 1.00. **So the §4b worry that the proxy is low is neither confirmed nor removed**; it still needs TLS or a canopy imager, or the Freiburg/Sydney comparisons above.
- **Allometric cross-check of the proxy (7 Nov; `scripts/allometric_lai.py` → `databases/barcelona/porta_allometric_lai.csv`, `porta_allometric_lai_species.csv`, `runoff_allometric_lai_porta.csv`).** i-Tree leaf-area equation for open-grown deciduous urban trees (Nowak 1996, Forest Science 42(4):504–507) on the LiDAR crown height and diameter of the 2,600 Porta trees, with the exp(MSE/2) bias correction (×1.123). Shading factor S from Nowak's Table 2 where listed (*Platanus* 0.86, *Celtis australis* 0.92, outside the stated 0.67–0.88 range, *Styphnolobium* 0.78, *Gleditsia* 0.67); otherwise 0.83, "the average shading coefficient for deciduous trees" (i-Tree UFORE Methods, citing Nowak 1996; checked 7 Nov). Height:width is held inside 0.5–2 as in UFORE; how the out-of-range crown is scaled back is an ASSUMPTION (UFORE scales height or width down; here the diameter is kept and leaf area is scaled by the height ratio). Nowak warns that the equation overestimates pruned trees, so for street trees it is an upper bound. **Result:** the allometric LAI is 2–3× the proxy (median *Platanus* 6.17 vs 2.31, and Escapos 2.51; *Celtis* 7.47 vs 2.00), and it barely agrees with the proxy tree by tree (Pearson 0.18, Spearman 0.28) or across species medians (Spearman 0.53, 25 species with n ≥ 15). The species order is driven mainly by S: one step from 0.83 to 0.92 multiplies leaf area by 1.67. Tall narrow crowns (*Populus*, *Casuarina*, *Fraxinus*) reach LAI ≈ 9 because the equation grows exponentially with crown size. H:D was clamped on 27% of trees. **So it does not replace the proxy; it serves as an upper-bound sensitivity.** Runoff with layouts held fixed (s = 1.75, T = 2 and 10 yr, 60 min): S0 interception rises from 794 to about 1,270 m³. S4 still beats S1a, by roughly twice the margin (T2 best-summer: −61.4 m³, −0.35%, vs −31.4 m³, −0.18%). Against today (S0) the sign flips from slightly worse to slightly better (T2: −10.5 m³ vs +22.7 m³). Every difference stays below 1%, so the runoff answer stays 'not resolved'.
- **Known limits:** the LiDAR LAI proxy (median ~2.3) is probably lower than ground-measured LAI (Freiburg, LAI-2200C optical canopy analyser: 1.4–4.9 per tree, 13 of 16 at ≥ 2.5; Anys & Weiler 2024; corrected 26 Oct, earlier "TLS 2.6–4.9" was wrong), so storage is likely underestimated. The bias may differ between species. Field LAI from a canopy imager in Sydney (Kaluarachchi et al. 2020) is 2.8 for *Platanus* (Porta proxy 2.31) and 2.6 for *Pyrus calleryana* (proxy 2.08), but only 2.0 for *Jacaranda* (proxy 2.91). The cities and methods differ, so treat this as a check of direction, not a correction; the same s is used for every species (species differ only through crown size and LAI); no routing, sewer or ponding (volume only); tree pits are not yet modelled separately.

## 5. Heat module (Ladybug Tools in Grasshopper now, Infrared City API later)
**Tier 1, fast, used for many layouts:** point-based UTCI at 1.1 m on a 2 m sidewalk grid.
`LB Import EPW` → hours → `LB Human to Sky Relation` (test points + context: buildings + tree crowns) → `LB Outdoor Solar MRT` → `LB UTCI Comfort` (air temperature, RH, wind from the EPW).
Limit: crowns act as opaque, so species differences enter only through crown size and shape, and shade MRT is underestimated (>6 °C below globe measurements under one tree). Fix, as in Peng et al. (2026): multiply direct radiation under the crown by τ = exp(−0.5·LAI) in the SolarCal step. This brought shade MRT within 2–4 °C of measurement. Ladybug also treats trees as shade only (no evapotranspiration), so it likely underestimates vegetation cooling by ~2–3 °C UTCI (Mannucci et al. 2025).
**Tier 2, detailed check of a few layouts:** `HB UTCI Comfort Map` (Honeybee: Radiance + EnergyPlus), with trees as HB Shades carrying a Radiance transmittance modifier and an energy transmittance schedule (leaf-on/off) from τ. Run locally or on Pollination.
**Later: Infrared City API** (UTCI/MRT in seconds) replaces Tier 1 inside the optimisation loop. Questions for the tutor are in `topic_decision.md` §6.4.
- **Metrics:** mean UTCI on sun-exposed sidewalks at the design hour; share of points in **strong heat stress (UTCI > 32 °C)** or above (stress classes: Bröde et al. 2012 *(to add)*); hours > 32 °C over the hot week (Tier 2).
- **Known limits:** the EPW is from the airport, not the city centre (test +1/+2 °C air-temperature offsets as sensitivity); UTCI uses wind at 10 m; transpiration cooling of air is not represented in Tier 1 (state it; Park 2026 shows it can exceed shading for air temperature). The omission matters less for Porta: in a meta-analysis of 26 field studies, transpiration per leaf area was lowest in Mediterranean climates (0.55 vs 6.96 g m⁻² min⁻¹ in Oceanic ones) and ~10× lower for trees in paved pits than over grass, while shade cooling of surfaces was largest over asphalt (Rahman et al. 2019; few Mediterranean studies, so possible bias). A fixed summer LAI overstates plane trees in heatwaves above ~40 °C (rare in Barcelona so far): Platanus lost roughly 30–50% of its plant area index after four days at 41.7–43.9 °C in Melbourne (Sanusi & Livesley 2020), so the plane baseline gets a low-LAI variant in the sensitivity runs. The design hour (day) misses night effects: dense tree clusters trap heat at night (Zölch et al. 2019); report it as a limit.
- **Validation:** Ladybug vs ENVI-met in a Mediterranean square: UTCI MBE 0.8–2.3 °C, CVRMSE 7–13%; MRT worse under direct sun (CVRMSE 22–38%) (Mannucci et al. 2025). Tier 1 vs Tier 2 on the same layouts; optional MRT spot measurements in Porta (globe thermometer) on a hot day.

### 5b. First UTCI checks of the tool layout (2–3 Nov 2026: Pg Andreu Nin and all of Porta, Tier 1 and Tier 2)
Run by the user in Grasshopper: 1,974 points at 1.1 m, 15 July, EPW 11–16 h (= 12–17 h CEST), El Prat TMYx, opaque crowns, 80 street positions. **First run superseded (sphere bug):** in the GH loader a non-uniform scale on `rg.Sphere(...).ToBrep()` left the crowns round (vertical extent = diameter), which made the flat S4 crowns ~3–4 m too tall; that run gave S0 27.79 °C, S4 28.24 °C (`utci_*_20261102.csv`, kept for the record). **Rerun with mesh crowns** (`utci_*_mesh_20261102.csv`, loader fixed in 0d4e96e): **S0 27.45 °C, S4 27.58 °C, S4 − S0 = +0.12 °C** (median +0.09; S4 warmer at 53% of points). 15 July in this TMYx file is an ordinary July day, not the hot design day.
Proxy re-computed at the same points and hours (`scripts/compare_ladybug_proxy.py` → `databases/barcelona/ladybug_vs_proxy_andreu_nin.csv`):
- **The proxy ranks points as Ladybug does** (mesh rerun). Across points in S0, proxy shade vs UTCI r = −0.73; on the S4 − S0 change, r = −0.82 with opaque crowns (−0.76 with LAI opacity). Slope ≈ −0.5 °C UTCI per hour of full shade.
- **Direct shade explains most of the difference.** With opaque crowns (as in Ladybug) the proxy gives S4 98.5% of S0's shade; at the 747 points where the proxy shade does not change, Ladybug gives +0.10 °C (it was +0.43 °C on the superseded sphere run). The small residual is plausibly sky view and longwave exchange, which the proxy does not model.
- **The LAI advantage is not tested by this run.** With LAI opacity the proxy gives S4 113% of S0's shade (S4 LAI 2.70 vs 2.08), but Ladybug treated all crowns as opaque. The τ = exp(−0.5·LAI) fix above (Tier 1) or Tier 2 / Infrared City is needed before the sign of S4 − S0 can be stated.

**Porous crowns in Python (route 1, 2 Nov; `scripts/porous_utci.py` → `databases/barcelona/porous_utci_andreu_nin.csv`).** Same chain and inputs as the Grasshopper run (ladybug-comfort `OutdoorSolarCal` + `UTCI`, Tregenza sky patches, one human point 0.918 m above the test point, ground reflectance 0.25), with the ray tests done in Python so a crown passes τ = exp(−0.5·LAI) (ASSUMPTION: the same τ for direct sun and sky view). Infrared City may not help here: the InFraReD papers read so far (2020–2022) represent green as area only, and the user recalls trees as plain polyhedra; to confirm with the developers.
- **Check against Grasshopper (mesh rerun), opaque crowns:** mean bias −0.02 °C, MAE 0.02 °C, per point r = 0.998 for both S0 and S4, r = 0.993 on the S4 − S0 change. The Python chain reproduces Ladybug, so its porous-crown and Porta-wide results can be used without a Grasshopper run each time. (Against the superseded sphere run the bias was −0.36 / −0.69 °C; the sphere bug was the cause.)
- **Consequence:** the opaque-crown Ladybug result and the proxy agree in sign and nearly in size on this street (+0.12 °C vs 98.5% of S0's shade).
- **Porous crowns:** S0 28.39 °C, S4 28.20 °C, **S4 − S0 = −0.19 °C** (median −0.17; p10 −0.92, p90 +0.57; S4 warmer at 33% of points). Porosity warms both scenarios (+0.96 °C S0, +0.64 °C S4), less for S4 because of its higher LAI.
- **All of Porta's plane streets** (`exchange/to_gh/ladybug_porta_planes_20261102/`, 832 positions, 21,352 points; `databases/barcelona/porous_utci_porta.csv`): S4 − S0 = **−0.04 °C opaque, −0.11 °C porous** (median +0.01 / −0.05; p10 −1.1, p90 +1.0 / +0.7; S4 warmer at 47% / 44% of points). The user's Grasshopper run on every 5th point (4,271 points, mesh crowns, `.../from_gh/utci_S{0,4}.csv`) gives S0 26.99, S4 26.95 °C; the Python chain at the same points 26.97 / 26.94 °C (bias −0.01 °C, r = 0.999). Pg Andreu Nin was one of the less favourable streets for S4.
- **Weather file.** The runs above used the full-record TMYx (1973–2023, July from 2014) that the Grasshopper kit downloaded (`..._TMYx.zip`), not the project's TMYx 2011–2025 (July from 2017; `..._TMYx.2011-2025.zip`), which on 15 July 11–16 h is 2.5–3 °C warmer in air with 100–170 W/m² more direct normal radiation. Rerun with TMYx 2011–2025 (OMEN, `*_epw2011.csv`): Pg Andreu Nin S0 29.93 / S4 30.02 °C opaque (+0.10), 30.80 / 30.61 °C porous (−0.19); all of Porta 29.53 / 29.50 °C opaque (−0.03), 30.19 / 30.09 °C porous (−0.10). The S4 − S0 differences do not change with the weather file. **TMYx 2011–2025 is the reference from now on.**
- **Tier 2, Honeybee** (`scripts/make_hb_model.py` → `exchange/to_gh/hb_andreu_nin_*.hbjson`; `utci_comfort_map` recipe, lbt-recipes 0.26.23, run on the OMEN with `scripts/run_hb_utci.py`; `databases/barcelona/hb_utci_andreu_nin*.csv`): crowns as ellipsoid shade meshes, porous ones with a Radiance glass modifier √τ per surface (two surfaces per ray, ASSUMPTION: no scattering inside the crown); no Honeybee rooms, so buildings and ground are at air temperature, as in Tier 1. TMYx 2011–2025: S0 30.81 / S4 30.87 °C opaque (**+0.06**), 31.35 / 31.19 °C porous (**−0.15**). Same weather as the Tier 1 runs: +0.07 and −0.17; per point the S4 − S0 change correlates with Tier 1 at r = 0.80–0.82. On the same weather Honeybee is warmer than Tier 1 in absolute terms (S0: +0.9 °C opaque, +0.6 °C porous); cause not checked (candidates: Radiance ground and sky treatment, sensor at 1.1 m vs Ladybug's body point at ~2.0 m).
- **Reading:** on this street and over all of Porta the S0–S4 difference is within ±0.2 °C on average (per point roughly ±1 °C), and its sign depends on whether crowns are opaque or porous. The defensible statement for the tool is "no worse than today's planes within model uncertainty", not "cooler". The tool's case rests on the other objectives (runoff, diversity under the city cap, winter sun), with heat as a constraint that holds.

## 6. Optimisation
- **Decision variables:** species at each replaced plane position (from the feasible palette); optionally, new positions. Since v6 (`notes/topic_decision.md` §9.7): planes on large avenues and emblematic places are kept by the city (2011 guide p. 17), so they leave the decision set once a rule defines them; the method must also work for any subset of positions (replacement happens at death or street works).
- **Objectives:** f₁ = heat (Tier 1 UTCI metric, or a calibrated shade × τ proxy pre-computed per position), f₂ = runoff volume at T = 2 yr (also reported for T = 1 and 10).
- **Constraints:** no species >15% within the study area (`ASSUMPTION`: a local reading of the plan's city-wide cap; alternatives in `notes/topic_decision.md` §9.7); species size class = the class the 2011 guide sets for the sidewalk width (pp. 22–23, one class per sidewalk type; allowing smaller classes on wider sidewalks would be an `ASSUMPTION`; needs sidewalk widths per position, `notes/sidewalk_width.md`); diversity floor (Shannon; optional in the tool since 12 Oct, set as an effective number of species exp(H) among the replanted positions, `shade_sponge/README.md`); excluded species (invasive, pest hosts); crown clearance from façades.
- **Algorithm:** NSGA-II, in Python with `pymoo` (fast proxies) or in Grasshopper with Wallacei. The final Pareto layouts are re-checked with Tier 1/Tier 2 UTCI.
- **Implemented v0 (8 Oct, `shade_sponge/`):** weighted-sum transportation LP instead of NSGA-II, with the shade × τ heat proxy, a topography-aware runoff target (runoff reaching flood hotspots, via DTM flow routing) and façade clearance. First result: no trade-off within the city palette (the front collapses). Adding the leaf-on calendar and season-weighted storms (Esbrí et al. 2026) does not change this: 78% of intense storm days fall in May–Oct, when all palette species are in full leaf. **Winter sun access** (winter shade on sunlit open ground as a cost, 15 Jan 10–15 h; leafless crowns cut ~35% of sunlight) creates the first real trade-off: beyond the balanced layout, winter sun costs summer shade. Re-run 15 Oct with seven species and the photo-based leaf calendar (Jacaranda mostly in leaf in January; `notes/decisions/leaf_state_inat.md`): the no-regret step summer-only → balanced (~90 Tipuana → Jacaranda) cuts winter shade by only 2% at equal summer shade (−2.3% in the 20 Oct re-run, which adds leaf calendars for Porta's kept trees of the shortlist species (about 92% of its LiDAR-measured non-plane trees; the other kept species, e.g. Morus alba, stay in full leaf, ASSUMPTION)), and the swap vanishes in 3 of 8 sensitivity variants (Tipuana bare in Jan–Feb, design day 21 Dec or 15 Feb). The +16% summer gain vs a random palette and the trade-off beyond balanced are robust (winter-heavy vs balanced: winter shade −14 to −23%, summer shade −17 to −20%; the winter-heavy layout uses the winter-bare Pyrus and Styphnolobium instead of Tipuana). Superseded (old calendar, Jacaranda bare Jan–Mar, 8 Oct): no-regret gain −9%, −2 to −12% across variants. Tipuana's winter leaf state: Santos Nouri et al. (2018) model Tipuana in Lisbon as leafed in winter (leafless only in early spring in Iberia, as cited), with PET up to 2.7 °C lower under the crown at noon in December. The leaf state is an input cited from Viñas & Solanich, not observed, so it is consistent with the tool's base case (leafed in January); Barcelona iNaturalist photos confirm it (all 20 determinable January photos in leaf; `notes/decisions/leaf_state_inat.md`). Field support for the winter term: in Coimbra (Csa), clear-winter midday air under leafless deciduous crowns was occasionally 0.3–0.7 °C warmer than under evergreens (Rochette Cordeiro et al. 2026). **Barcelona shortlist (9 Oct, re-run 20 Oct):** 34 species common in Barcelona's streets, crowns from LiDAR on 3 tiles (one shared with Porta); optimised layouts then match the mature planes on summer shade (+0.9% balanced) and the summer–winter front widens, but a few species dominate (Ulmus pumila + Pinus pinea = 84% of positions in the balanced layout). **Optional diversity floor (12 Oct):** at least N effective species, exp(Shannon H) as in SylvCiT, linearised inside the LP; N = 10 brings those two species down to 52% for −2.0% summer shade. Details and limits in `shade_sponge/README.md`.
- **Surrogate precedent (12 Oct):** in an evolutionary search with a graph-network surrogate, the best architecture found reaches a mean test accuracy of 94.22% on NAS-Bench-101 after 1,000 queries (architecture evaluations) (Xue et al. 2026, matrix #90, abstract). Same motivation as our proxies (fewer true evaluations per search); the mechanism differs, since their surrogate is trained online and our shade × τ proxy is analytic.
- **Why proxies are needed:** Ladybug in the loop costs 20–25 min per layout for 42 trees (Shaamala et al. 2025). On a 25 × 36 m site it costs 10–20 s rising to 2–3 h for 200 evaluations (Peng et al. 2026). Porta's 832 plane positions need a pre-computed shade × τ proxy, a surrogate, or the Infrared City API. Precedent for the Python stack: PySWMM + pymoo NSGA-II, population 200 × 120 generations on 50 parallel processes for 520 green-infrastructure area variables (Liu et al. 2026, no trees). Tree-placement precedents: a genetic algorithm needed 7 h per run for ~730 layouts and moved site-mean UTCI by only 0.1–0.3 °C (Hao et al. 2023); an SVR trained on 314,928 Ladybug shading runs predicted the best tree configuration per canyon with R² = 0.63, lower on held-out data (Oneto et al. 2026). Expect small per-layout differences and test them against the proxy error. Closest precedent (Tan & Liu 2026): from 1,500 radiation + CFD runs (1,250 training, 250 validation), a Random Forest surrogate (RMSE 0.09 °C site-mean UTCI) drove NSGA-II over species and positions of 30 trees in ~5 min per run after the offline sampling (sampling cost not reported), with Pareto solutions re-simulated; species and placement together gave a 0.55–1.32 °C spread among representative layouts (0.3 °C for placement only in Hao et al. 2023). This is the template for replacing Tier 1 inside the loop.

## 12. Proposed changes (13 Oct 2026, not adopted)
Drawn from the papers read in full on 12–13 Oct. Nothing below is applied to the code or to the sections above; each item needs the user's decision. Matrix ids refer to `notes/lit/literature_matrix.csv`.

1. **Weight the heat proxy by ground surface.** Today the proxy counts effective shade on all sunlit open ground alike (`shade_sponge/heat.py`).
   - **Evidence:** the same shade cools dark sealed ground (asphalt, dark paving) about twice as much as grass; light paving cools about as much as grass. Per unit LAI, surface cooling is about 6 °C over asphalt vs 3 °C over grass (Rahman 2019 #102). Shade lowered black tiles by 28.1 °C, white tiles by 11.4 °C and natural grass by 12.9 °C (Sharmin 2023 #104).
   - **Change:** multiply shaded area by a surface factor from the 1 m land-cover grid already used by the runoff module: sealed = 1, pervious = 0.5 (dark sealing assumed, since the grid does not separate light paving). Mark the factor as an ASSUMPTION and test 0.4–0.6.
   - **Effect:** shifts trees towards sealed sidewalks and squares, the same places where interception counts for runoff (§4b). This could strengthen the no-trade-off result rather than change it.
2. **Check, rather than add, a sky-view-factor rule.** The proxy already counts shade only where buildings leave pedestrians in the sun, which is the mechanism behind the published placement rules.
   - **Evidence:** plant first where SVF > 0.5, on the sun-exposed side of east–west streets (Coutts #100). Optimised added trees went mainly to east–west streets and large paved spaces. Relocating existing trees from already-shaded spots to sealed plazas and parking lots cut hours above 60 °C Tmrt by 19.7% (Schrodi 2023 #87).
   - **Change:** report SVF and street orientation for the chosen positions as a plausibility check of the optimiser. Add an SVF term only if the chosen positions contradict these rules.
3. **Mark the extinction coefficient as an assumption.** `heat.py` uses K = 0.5 (Beer–Lambert, as in Peng et al. 2026) without an ASSUMPTION tag.
   - **Evidence:** K bundles the leaf-angle factor G. Species leaf-angle differences changed MRT by up to 9.5 °C, and a uniform leaf-angle assumption by up to 5.7 °C (Li 2023 #95).
   - **Change:** tag K as an ASSUMPTION in `heat.py` and §3, and add a sensitivity run. The test range has no source yet and must be set and cited first.
4. **Keep R_OFF = 0.5 for leafless storage, add the new anchors, and report storm-size limits.** `season.py` already gives leafless crowns 50% of full-leaf storage (tested 0.3–0.7).
   - **Anchors:**
     - Birch storage capacity was 1.01 mm leafless vs 2.61 mm leafed in the text (2.65 mm in Table 1), a ratio of 0.38–0.39 (Alivio 2025 #117).
     - Birch event interception was 32% leafless vs 51% leafed, a ratio of 0.63 (Zabret 2018 #116).
     - Both birch ratios sit inside the 0.3–0.7 test range. Pine (3.04 vs 4.01 mm, 0.76) is evergreen and is not an anchor for leafless crowns.
     - Leafless deciduous trees still intercepted or channelled nearly 20% of rain to the tree base in a meta-analysis (Nooraei Beidokhti 2021 #126); this is a share of rain, not a ratio.
   - **Change:** report runoff gains mainly for frequent storms (T = 1–2 yr), and state that species differences shrink in intense storms. Evidence: tree type stopped mattering above 7.6 mm/h (Nytch 2019 #110), and canopy interception fell below 5% of the water balance above 60 mm (Alivio 2025 #117). This supports H3 as stated.
5. **Add a storage anchor to the s sensitivity range.**
   - **Evidence:** measured storage capacity per unit LAI is about 1.00–1.02 mm (birch; text vs Table 1) and 1.08 mm (pine) when leafed (own calculation from Alivio 2025 #117). Both fall inside the current s range of 0.86–2.2 mm, below the calibrated 1.75 mm. They are storage capacities (Pereira method), not effective storage including evaporation during events, so they belong with the lower-bound evidence in §4b.
   - **Change:** cite them in §4b. Keep s = 1.75 as the base case.
6. **Context for S0/S1, no change.** Platanus × acerifolia is 27.6% of Barcelona's 145,176 street trees (`databases/barcelona/arbrat_viari.csv`). In Philadelphia, Dong et al. classed it, from i-Tree Eco values, as Pareto-dominated ('functionally inferior'): worse than at least one other species on all four services (stormwater, energy-proxy cooling, air quality, carbon) (Dong 2026 #85). This supports replacement as a scenario, but the metrics and city differ, so it is not evidence for Porta.
